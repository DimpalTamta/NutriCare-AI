# Healthy Eating Principles

## Introduction
*   A healthy diet for cancer patients and survivors is a specialized way of eating that focuses on providing the body with the resources it needs to heal.
*   It may differ from general healthy eating guidelines because the primary goal shifts toward supporting **physical strength**, maintaining a robust **immune system**, and accelerating **recovery** from treatment.
*   While typical healthy diets often focus on restriction or weight loss, a cancer-supportive diet prioritizes nutrient density and meeting the body's increased metabolic demands during and after care.

## What is a Healthy Diet?
*   A healthy diet in the context of cancer care provides adequate **nutrients, energy (calories), and hydration** to support all bodily functions.
*   It includes a wide variety of foods that work together to help the body repair tissues damaged by treatment and prevent the onset of clinical malnutrition.
*   By ensuring the body has a steady supply of fuel, a healthy diet helps patients tolerate therapies better and maintain a higher quality of life.

## Healthy Plate Model
*   The plate model is a visual guide where **half the plate** is filled with colorful vegetables and fruits.
*   One **quarter of the plate** should consist of lean protein sources, and the final **quarter** should be dedicated to whole grains.
*   For patients with small appetites or early fullness, portion sizes can be reduced while keeping the proportions the same, or the model can be used to guide smaller, more frequent "mini-meals".

## Choose Whole Foods
*   Whole foods are prioritized over highly processed options because they remain close to their natural state and retain a higher concentration of nutrients.
*   Examples include fresh vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, and fresh meats or plant-based proteins.
*   Minimal processing ensures that the food’s natural vitamins, minerals, and enzymes are preserved to best support the body's recovery.

## Eat a Variety of Foods
*   Eating a variety of foods is essential because different food groups provide unique sets of nutrients that the body cannot get from a single source.
*   Aiming for a **"rainbow" of colors** on your plate ensures a broad spectrum of vitamins, minerals, and protective antioxidants.
*   This diversity helps support the gut microbiome, which plays a vital role in overall health and treatment response.

## Increase Fruits and Vegetables
*   It is generally recommended to aim for at least **five servings** of fruits and vegetables daily.
*   These foods are powerhouses of vitamins, minerals, and fiber, which help regulate the digestive system and protect healthy cells.
*   If your appetite is low, try incorporating them into smoothies, soups, or purees to make them easier to consume without needing to chew large volumes.

## Choose Whole Grains
*   Whole grains include foods like **brown rice, oats, whole wheat, quinoa, and barley**, which contain the entire grain kernel.
*   Unlike refined grains (like white bread or white rice), whole grains are rich in fiber and essential B vitamins.
*   Dietary fiber is an "unsung nutrient" that feeds the microbiome and helps maintain regular bowel movements, though many patients do not get enough.

## Healthy Protein Choices
*   Lean protein sources such as **poultry, fish, eggs, beans, lentils, and tofu** are vital for repairing tissues and maintaining muscle mass.
*   Plant-based proteins are excellent choices as they often come with added fiber and phytochemicals.
*   During active treatment, cancer patients may need extra protein; therefore, full-fat dairy or higher-calorie protein options may be appropriate to prevent weight loss.

## Healthy Fats
*   Unsaturated fats are "healthy fats" that support heart health and provide a concentrated source of energy.
*   Excellent sources include **olive oil, avocados, nuts, seeds, and fatty fish** like salmon.
*   Because fats are calorie-dense, adding them to meals is a helpful strategy for patients struggling to maintain their weight during treatment.

## Limit Sugar
*   Added sugars—found in soda, candy, and many processed snacks—provide "empty calories" that lack the nutrients needed for healing.
*   High intake of added sugar can contribute to unintended weight gain and may cause spikes in blood sugar levels.
*   It is important to differentiate these from the natural sugars found in whole fruits, which are accompanied by beneficial fiber and vitamins.

## Limit Salt
*   High sodium intake can lead to fluid retention and is generally not ideal for overall health.
*   Processed and "ready-to-eat" foods are the primary sources of hidden salt in most diets.
*   To maintain flavor without adding salt, try using fresh herbs, lemon juice, or spices to enhance the taste of your meals.

## Limit Processed Foods
*   Processed foods are often problematic because they are high in added sugars, salt, and unhealthy fats while being low in actual nutrients.
*   Choosing fresh or minimally processed alternatives (like frozen vegetables without added sauces) provides better support for the body’s needs.
*   Reducing these foods helps lower inflammation and supports a healthier environment for the body to recover.

## Meal Timing
*   Eating **small, frequent meals** (six to eight per day) is often much better tolerated than trying to eat three large meals.
*   Try to eat when your appetite is strongest, which for many patients is in the morning.
*   Keep healthy, calorie-dense snacks readily available and try to listen to your body's hunger cues, even if they occur at non-traditional times.

## Key Facts
*   **1 in 3 men and women** will be diagnosed with cancer in their lifetime, making nutritional support a widespread necessity.
*   There are more than **18 million cancer survivors** currently alive in the US today who have navigated these dietary changes.
*   Maintaining a healthy weight and staying active, such as through regular walking, can significantly reduce cancer-related fatigue.
*   Dietary fiber is essential for feeding the microbiome, yet most people consume far less than the recommended daily amount.
*   Proper nutrition is recognized as a fundamental part of the cancer journey that supports better tolerance of intensive medical therapies.
*   Nutritional care is increasingly recognized as a human right for all patients undergoing cancer treatment.
