# Understanding Cancer

## Overview
**Cancer** is a leading cause of death worldwide, accounting for nearly 10 million deaths in 2024, which is approximately one in six deaths. It is a generic term describing a large group of diseases that can affect any part of the body. Understanding the nature of cancer is essential for measuring its burden on society and identifying areas where medical progress is most needed.Cancer is a group of diseases that can affect almost any part of the body. It is one of the leading causes of death worldwide, accounting for nearly 10 million deaths in 2024. Understanding how cancer develops, its causes, and its risk factors is essential for improving awareness and supporting early detection.

## What is Cancer?
Cancer is not a single disease, but a collection of related diseases that can occur almost anywhere in the body.
*   **Malignant Tumours / Neoplasms:** These are other medical terms used interchangeably with cancer.
*   **Disease of Genes:** At its most basic level, cancer is a disease of the **genes** in our cells that control how they function.
*   **Abnormal Growth:** It involves the rapid production of abnormal cells that grow beyond their typical boundaries.
*   **Uncontrolled Division:** Changes to genes cause cells to malfunction, leading them to grow and divide when they should not—or preventing them from dying when they should.

## How Cancer Develops
The development of cancer is a multi-stage process where normal cells transform into tumour cells.
*   **Normal Cell Transformation:** This progression generally moves from a pre-cancerous lesion to a **malignant tumour**.
*   **Genetic Mutation:** Changes to genes disrupt the normal signals that manage cell work and survival.
*   **Cell Cycle Disruption:** Genetic mutations cause a breakdown in the normal signals that control how cells grow and divide.

## What Causes Cancer?
Cancer arises from the interaction between an individual's genetic factors and exposure to three categories of external agents known as **carcinogens**:

### Physical Carcinogens
*   Ultraviolet (UV) radiation.
*   Ionizing radiation.

### Chemical Carcinogens
*   Asbestos.
*   Components of tobacco smoke.
*   Alcohol.
*   Aflatoxin (a food contaminant).
*   Arsenic (a drinking water contaminant).

### Biological Carcinogens
*   Infections from certain viruses, such as **human papillomavirus (HPV)** and **hepatitis**.
*   Bacteria, such as *Helicobacter pylori*.
*   Specific parasites.

## Common Risk Factors
Key modifiable and non-modifiable factors contribute to cancer risk:
*   **Tobacco and Alcohol:** Tobacco use and alcohol consumption are major global risk factors.
*   **Diet and Physical Inactivity:** An unhealthy diet, low fruit and vegetable intake, and a lack of physical activity.
*   **Obesity:** High body mass index (BMI) is a significant contributor to cancer deaths.
*   **Air Pollution:** An important environmental risk factor, particularly for lung cancer.
*   **Chronic Infections:** Approximately 10% of global cancer diagnoses are attributed to carcinogenic infections, including HPV, hepatitis B and C, and *Helicobacter pylori*.
*   **Age:** Cancer incidence rises dramatically with age, likely due to the accumulation of risk factors and the decreasing effectiveness of cellular repair mechanisms.

## What is Metastasis?
**Metastasis** is a defining feature of cancer and is the primary cause of death from the disease.
*   **Definition:** The rapid creation of abnormal cells that grow beyond their usual boundaries.
*   **Spread:** These cells can invade adjoining parts of the body and spread to distant organs.

## Why Early Detection Matters
Cancer mortality is significantly reduced when cases are detected and treated at an early stage.
*   **Better Treatment Response:** When caught early, cancer is more likely to respond to treatment.
*   **Improved Survival:** Early diagnosis leads to a greater probability of survival and less morbidity.
*   **Lower Complexity:** Treatment for early-stage cancer is generally less expensive and less complex than for advanced disease.
*   **Quality of Life:** Detecting cancer early and avoiding delays in care significantly improves a patient's quality of life.

## Key Facts
*   Cancer can affect almost any part of the body and spreads through the process of metastasis.
*   It develops when abnormal cells grow uncontrollably due to genetic malfunctions.
*   Approximately **38% of cancers** can be prevented by avoiding known risk factors.
*   Nearly a quarter of cancer deaths are linked to modifiable risks like tobacco, alcohol, and poor diet.
*   Many cancers have a high chance of cure if they are detected early and treated effectively.
