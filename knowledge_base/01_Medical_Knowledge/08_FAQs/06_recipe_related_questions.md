# 06_Recipe_Related_Questions.md

**Q: What can I cook with rice and paneer?**
*   Yes, you can make a soft rice and paneer pilaf using gentle cooking methods like boiling [Turn 1].
*   Paneer is an excellent high-protein dairy choice that helps your body rebuild tissues [Turn 5, 87].
*   Rice provides the energy (calories) needed to maintain a healthy weight [28, Turn 5].

**Practical Tip:** Use mild herbs like parsley or cumin instead of hot chilies to avoid irritating your mouth [Turn 3].

**Safety Note:** Ensure the paneer is made from pasteurized milk to prevent infections [Turn 4].

**Q: What can I make with spinach?**
*   Yes, you can steam or boil spinach until it is very tender and easy to chew [Turn 1].
*   It is a great addition to a pureed diet when blended into a smooth soup with cream or high-protein yogurt [Turn 2, Turn 5].

**Practical Tip:** Pureeing spinach into a "smoothie" or soup is helpful if you have swallowing problems [32, Turn 2].

**Safety Note:** Always wash fresh spinach thoroughly under running water to remove dirt and bacteria [Turn 4].

**Q: Suggest a high-protein breakfast.**
*   Yes, scrambled eggs, Greek yogurt, or a protein-fortified oatmeal are great starts to your day [Turn 2, Turn 5].
*   High-protein foods are vital for repairing tissues that treatment may harm [29, Turn 5].

**Practical Tip:** Add a scoop of nut butter or pasteurized cheese to your eggs for even more protein [Turn 2, Turn 4].

**Safety Note:** Cook eggs until both the white and yolk are firm to ensure they are safe to eat [Turn 4].

**Q: Suggest a high-calorie lunch.**
*   Yes, creamy pasta with chicken or a thick vegetable stew with added olive oil provides necessary energy [29, Turn 4].
*   Adding healthy fats is a primary way to stop undesirable weight loss.

**Practical Tip:** Use extra gravies or sauces to make the meal moister and easier to swallow [32, Turn 2].

**Q: Suggest a soft dinner.**
*   Yes, flaky steamed fish with mashed potatoes and well-cooked carrots fits a soft diet perfectly [Turn 2].
*   Soft foods require very little chewing and are gentle on a sore mouth or throat [32, Turn 2].

**Practical Tip:** Use a fork to ensure all ingredients can be easily mashed before eating [Turn 2].

**Q: Suggest a liquid meal.**
*   Yes, a high-protein smoothie made with milk, soft fruit, and protein powder is an ideal liquid option [Turn 2, Turn 5].
*   This is helpful on days when you cannot tolerate any solid textures [Turn 2].

**Practical Tip:** Drink liquid meals slowly through a straw to avoid feeling full or bloated too quickly [Turn 3, Turn 5].

**Q: Suggest vegetarian recipes.**
*   Yes, lentil soups (dals) and bean-based stews are excellent plant-protein sources [Turn 5, 87].
*   These can be mashed or pureed to meet your specific texture needs [Turn 2].

**Practical Tip:** Add avocado or olive oil to these recipes to increase the calorie count [Turn 4].

**Q: Suggest vegan recipes.**
*   Yes, a soft tofu stir-fry with sautéed vegetables or a blended chickpea soup are nutrient-dense options [Turn 1, Turn 5].
*   Include a variety of plant proteins to support your body's healing process.

**Practical Tip:** Use nut butters or ground seeds to add healthy fats and extra protein to vegan meals [Turn 5].

**Q: Suggest Indian recipes.**
*   Yes, mild khichdi (rice and lentils) or paneer bhurji are nutritious and can be made very soft [Turn 2, Turn 5].
*   Indian cuisine offers many boiled and steamed options that are easy to digest [Turn 1].

**Practical Tip:** Use turmeric and ginger for mild flavor, but avoid "hot" red chili powder if you have mouth sores [Turn 3, Turn 4].

**Q: Suggest international recipes.**
*   Yes, a mild Mediterranean pasta with olive oil or a soft French omelet are good choices [Turn 1, Turn 5].
*   Stick to recipes that use boiling or baking rather than deep frying [Turn 1, Turn 4].

**Practical Tip:** Focus on recipes that allow you to easily add extra calories like butter or oils.

**Q: Suggest quick recipes.**
*   Yes, Greek yogurt with honey or a simple egg salad take very little energy to prepare [Turn 5].
*   Quick recipes are important for managing treatment-related fatigue [Turn 5, 61].

**Practical Tip:** Use pre-cut frozen vegetables or canned beans to save time in the kitchen [Turn 3, Turn 5].

**Q: Suggest low-spice meals.**
*   Yes, boiled chicken with white rice or plain poached fish are gentle on the system [Turn 1].
*   Low-spice foods are essential if you have mouth sores or a sore throat [Turn 4, 32].

**Practical Tip:** Use fresh herbs like basil or rosemary to add flavor without the sting of spices [Turn 3].

**Q: Suggest recipes for nausea.**
*   Yes, cold sandwiches, room-temperature pasta salads, or plain crackers are often best [33, Turn 3].
*   Cold foods have fewer strong odors that can trigger an upset stomach [Turn 3, 88].

**Practical Tip:** Stay away from the kitchen while food is being cooked to avoid triggering nausea [Turn 3].

**Q: Suggest recipes for diarrhea.**
*   Yes, stick to plain white rice, bananas, and white toast (the "BRAT" diet) [Turn 4, 33].
*   Drink clear broths or 100% fruit juices to stay hydrated [33, Turn 2].

**Safety Note:** Avoid greasy, fried, or high-fiber foods until your symptoms improve [Turn 4, 33].

**Q: Suggest recipes for constipation.**
*   Yes, high-fiber oatmeal with berries or a hearty bean soup can help move your bowels [33, Turn 4].
*   Fiber-rich foods are key to managing this common side effect.

**Practical Tip:** Increase your water intake significantly as you add more fiber to your diet [33, Turn 4].

**Q: Suggest recipes after chemotherapy.**
*   Yes, prioritize soft, moist, and bland foods that are easy for your body to digest [Turn 2, Turn 4].
*   Focus on getting enough protein to help your body recover from the systemic treatment [Turn 6, 29].

**Practical Tip:** Use plastic forks and spoons if you are experiencing a metallic taste in your mouth [32, Turn 3].

**Q: Suggest recipes after surgery.**
*   Yes, your body needs extra protein and calories for wound healing and tissue repair [31, Turn 5].
*   High-protein shakes or pureed vegetable soups are often easier to tolerate initially [31, Turn 2].

**Practical Tip:** Add healthy oils or butter to your meals to boost the calories needed for healing [29, Turn 5].

**Q: Suggest recipes using only my ingredients.**
*   Yes, the chatbot can match recipes specifically to what you have in your pantry to reduce stress [Turn 5].
*   The goal is to keep meal preparation simple and achievable [Turn 5].

**Practical Tip:** Ask about safe substitutions, like using yogurt instead of sour cream [Turn 5].

**Q: Suggest healthy snacks.**
*   Yes, cottage cheese, pudding, or small portions of nut butter on crackers are nutrient-dense [Turn 2, Turn 5].
*   Snacking frequently is a great way to meet your calorie goals if you have a low appetite [33, Turn 3].

**Practical Tip:** Keep high-protein snacks within easy reach so you can eat whenever you feel able [Turn 3, 33].

**Q: Show nutrition information for the recipe.**
*   Yes, the chatbot should display "Protein per serving" and "Total Calories" for every recipe [Turn 5, Turn 6].
*   This helps you track your goals and ensures you are getting the energy you need to heal [28, Turn 6].

**Safety Note:** Always review your nutritional goals with your oncology registered dietitian [27, Turn 6].
