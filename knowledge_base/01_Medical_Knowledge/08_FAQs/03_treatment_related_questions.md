# 03_Treatment_Related_Questions.md

**Q: What should I eat before chemotherapy?**
*   Yes, focus on eating high-calorie and high-protein foods to build up your strength.
*   A light, bland meal a few hours before your appointment often works best if you are prone to nausea.
*   Staying well-hydrated before you start is essential for your body to function correctly.

**Practical Tip:** Try a small bowl of oatmeal or toast with nut butter to keep your energy up without feeling too full.

**Safety Note:** If your doctor has given you specific fasting instructions for certain tests, always follow those first.

**Q: What should I eat after chemotherapy?**
*   Yes, choose soft and bland foods if you are experiencing nausea or mouth sores.
*   Continue to prioritize high-protein foods like eggs or yogurt to help rebuild body tissues harmed by treatment.
*   Eat whenever you feel hungry, even if it is not a traditional mealtime.

**Practical Tip:** Keep small, high-protein snacks like cheese sticks or protein crackers nearby for when your appetite returns.

**Q: Can I eat during chemotherapy?**
*   Yes, most patients can eat small snacks or light meals during their infusion to maintain energy.
*   Choose foods with low odors, as strong smells can sometimes trigger nausea in the treatment room.

**Practical Tip:** Bring easy-to-eat snacks like juice boxes, crackers, or a sandwich from home.

**Q: What should I eat before radiation?**
*   Yes, maintain a diet rich in protein and calories to help your body stay strong.
*   Drink plenty of fluids to stay hydrated, as radiation can sometimes be drying for the body.

**Practical Tip:** If your radiation is in the head or neck area, focus on very moist foods to prevent discomfort.

**Q: What should I eat after radiation?**
*   Yes, focus on a wide variety of foods to help your body heal and recover.
*   If you have a sore throat or mouth, switch to a soft or liquid diet to make eating more comfortable.

**Practical Tip:** Use gravies, sauces, or broths to moisten your food and make it easier to swallow.

**Q: What should I eat after surgery?**
*   Yes, your body needs extra protein and calories to heal wounds and repair tissues.
*   Start with clear liquids or soft foods as recommended by your surgical team.

**Practical Tip:** High-protein shakes can be an easy way to get the nutrients you need if you aren't ready for solid food yet.

**Q: What foods help healing?**
*   Yes, high-protein foods like lean meats, beans, eggs, and dairy are best for tissue repair.
*   Foods rich in vitamins and minerals, like fruits and vegetables, support your overall recovery.

**Practical Tip:** Add a scoop of protein powder to smoothies or soups to boost their healing power.

**Q: Can I eat before treatment?**
*   Yes, unless your doctor tells you otherwise (such as before surgery), it is important to eat to maintain your energy.
*   Eating a small meal can help you feel less weak during long treatment sessions.

**Safety Note:** Always confirm "NPO" (no food or drink) instructions with your surgical team at least 24 hours before a procedure.

**Q: Can I skip meals?**
*   No, skipping meals can lead to unplanned weight loss and make you feel much weaker.
*   If you don't feel like eating a full meal, try having a small snack or a high-calorie drink instead.

**Practical Tip:** Set a timer on your phone to remind you to eat every 2–3 hours.

**Q: What should I drink during treatment?**
*   Yes, drink plenty of water, broths, and 100% fruit juices to stay hydrated.
*   Fluids are essential for helping your kidneys process treatment medications.

**Practical Tip:** If plain water tastes metallic, try adding a slice of lemon or cucumber to change the flavor.

**Q: How much protein do I need?**
*   Yes, your protein needs increase during cancer treatment to help rebuild tissues.
*   The exact amount depends on your weight and type of treatment, so a registered dietitian can give you a specific goal.

**Practical Tip:** Aim to include a source of protein at every meal and snack.

**Q: What if I cannot eat?**
*   Yes, if side effects make eating impossible, your team may suggest tube feeding or intravenous (IV) nutrition.
*   These methods ensure you still get the calories and nutrients needed to survive and heal.

**Safety Note:** Contact your doctor immediately if you have not been able to eat or drink anything for 24 hours.

**Q: Should I eat small meals?**
*   Yes, eating 5–6 small meals a day is often easier to handle than 3 large ones.
*   This strategy helps you get enough calories without feeling overwhelmed or nauseated.

**Practical Tip:** Think of "mini-meals" like half a sandwich or a small bowl of soup.

**Q: Can I take supplements?**
*   Yes, but No—only take them after checking with your oncology team.
*   Some vitamins or herbal supplements can interfere with how your chemotherapy or radiation works.

**Safety Note:** High doses of certain antioxidants might reduce the effectiveness of radiation therapy.

**Q: Can I drink protein shakes?**
*   Yes, protein shakes and nutritional drinks are great tools to help you meet your calorie and protein goals.
*   They are especially helpful on days when you have a very low appetite.

**Practical Tip:** Drink shakes slowly through a straw to avoid feeling too full too quickly.

**Q: What foods help recovery?**
*   Yes, a wide variety of whole foods, including high-protein meats and healthy fats, support recovery.
*   Focus on nutrient-dense choices that help your body regain its strength.

**Practical Tip:** Focus on "food first" before turning to supplements for your nutrients.

**Q: Can I eat immediately after treatment?**
*   Yes, you can eat as soon as you feel up to it.
*   If you feel nauseated, wait until the feeling passes and start with small sips of clear liquids.

**Practical Tip:** Try a small piece of ginger candy to help settle your stomach after treatment.

**Q: How should I manage fatigue through diet?**
*   Yes, staying hydrated and eating small, frequent meals can help keep your energy levels steady.
*   Choose "easy-to-prepare" foods to save your energy for healing.

**Practical Tip:** Ask friends or family to help with meal prep or use pre-cut frozen vegetables to save time.

**Q: What foods should I avoid during treatment?**
*   Yes, avoid alcohol and tobacco, as they are major risk factors that can worsen your health.
*   Limit raw or undercooked foods to prevent infections while your immune system is weak.

**Safety Note:** Always wash fruits and vegetables thoroughly and avoid unpasteurized dairy products.

**Q: When should I contact my doctor?**
*   Yes, contact your doctor if you are losing weight without trying or cannot stop vomiting.
*   Reach out if you have severe diarrhea, constipation, or pain that prevents you from eating.

**Safety Note:** Do not wait for your next appointment if you are unable to drink enough fluids to stay hydrated.
