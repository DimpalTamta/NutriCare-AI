# Nutrition During Immunotherapy

## Introduction
*   Good nutrition is a vital part of your immunotherapy journey, helping your body stay strong and resilient.
*   Unlike chemotherapy, immunotherapy works with your immune system, making the health of your gut and overall nutrient intake even more important.
*   Proper nutritional care is recognized as a fundamental **human right** for all patients undergoing cancer treatment.

## What is Immunotherapy?
*   Immunotherapy is a treatment that uses the body's own immune system to recognize and attack cancer cells.
*   A common type is **checkpoint inhibitor therapy**, which helps immune cells see and destroy cancer more effectively.
*   It is often described as "precision oncology" because it leverages the body's biological defenses.

## How Immunotherapy Can Affect Eating
*   Immunotherapy can cause systemic inflammation as the immune system becomes more active.
*   Side effects are often "immune-related" and can affect the digestive tract, leading to changes in appetite or bowel habits.
*   Because it works through the immune system, the health of your **microbiome** (the good bacteria in your gut) can play a role in how you respond to treatment.

## Common Nutrition Challenges
*   **Fatigue:** Extreme tiredness is a frequent side effect; staying active with gentle **walking** can actually help boost your energy.
*   **Appetite Changes:** You may not feel as hungry as usual or may feel full very quickly.
*   **Digestive Issues:** Inflammation in the gut (colitis) can sometimes lead to diarrhea or stomach discomfort.
*   **Taste Changes:** Some patients report that foods taste different, which can make eating less enjoyable.

## Nutrition Goals
*   Maintain a stable weight and preserve muscle mass to support physical function.
*   Support the **gut microbiome** by consuming adequate dietary fiber.
*   Ensure the body has enough **protein and calories** to repair healthy cells and support immune activity.

## Recommended Foods
*   **High-Fiber Foods:** Beans, lentils, whole grains, and colorful vegetables feed the microbiome, which is essential during immunotherapy.
*   **Lean Proteins:** Eggs, poultry, fish, tofu, and legumes help maintain muscle mass and support tissue repair.
*   **Healthy Fats:** Avocados, olive oil, and nuts provide concentrated energy for weight maintenance.
*   **A "Rainbow" of Produce:** Different colored fruits and vegetables provide a variety of antioxidants and vitamins to support immunity.

## Foods to Limit
*   **Highly Processed Foods:** Limit foods high in added sugars and unhealthy fats, as these can promote inflammation.
*   **Raw or Undercooked Foods:** Avoid raw seafood, undercooked meats, or unpasteurized dairy to reduce the risk of infection while your immune system is being manipulated.
*   **Symptom Triggers:** If you experience gut inflammation or diarrhea, you may need to temporarily limit very spicy or greasy foods.

## Hydration
*   Sipping water, clear broths, or electrolyte drinks throughout the day is critical for flushing treatment byproducts.
*   Adequate hydration helps manage fatigue and supports kidney function during therapy.
*   Try to drink most of your fluids between meals to avoid feeling too full to eat solid food.

## Managing Side Effects Through Diet
*   **For Fatigue:** Combine small, frequent meals with light exercise like **walking** to improve energy levels.
*   **For Nausea:** Choose bland, room-temperature foods that have less aroma.
*   **For Gut Issues:** If diarrhea occurs, focus on a "low-residue" diet temporarily (like white rice or bananas) and stay well-hydrated.

## Meal Planning Tips
*   **Eat Small and Often:** Aim for 6–8 small snacks or "mini-meals" instead of three large ones to make eating feel less overwhelming.
*   **Be Proactive:** Prepare high-protein snacks in advance so they are ready when you have the most energy.
*   **Fiber Focus:** Gradually increase your fiber intake to reach recommended levels, as most people do not get nearly enough.

## Key Facts
*   **1 in 3 people** will be diagnosed with cancer in their lifetime.
*   There are over **18 million cancer survivors** currently alive in the US today.
*   **Malnutrition** affects between 20% and 80% of cancer patients and can negatively impact treatment outcomes.
*   Dietary fiber is an "unsung nutrient" that is vital for feeding the microbiome during immunotherapy.
*   The American Cancer Society has invested over **$5.6 billion in research** to improve patient support and survival.
