# Nutrition During Palliative Care

## Introduction
*   Nutrition in palliative care is a compassionate approach that prioritizes a patient's **comfort and quality of life**.
*   It shifts the focus from strict clinical targets to supporting the individual's well-being and daily enjoyment of food.
*   Providing proper nutritional care is recognized globally as a fundamental **human right** for all patients.

## What is Palliative Care?
*   Palliative care is a specialized form of medical support designed to **improve the lives** of people with cancer and their families.
*   It provides a "compassionate ear" and guidance at every step of the cancer journey, from diagnosis through end-of-life care.
*   The goal is to ensure that everyone has the opportunity to live as well as possible by addressing physical, emotional, and social needs.

## Goals of Nutrition During Palliative Care
*   The primary objective is to **alleviate nutrition-related symptoms** to enhance daily comfort.
*   Nutrition support aims to maintain the patient's physical strength and energy levels to support their independence.
*   It focuses on providing "nutrition with love" that respects the patient’s autonomy and personal wishes.

## Maintaining Comfort
*   Comfort is maintained by removing the pressure to meet specific caloric or weight goals.
*   Patients are encouraged to "eat right" based on what feels good to them in the moment, rather than following a restrictive diet.
*   The focus remains on **pleasure-based eating**, allowing the patient to enjoy their favorite flavors and textures.

## Managing Symptoms
*   Palliative nutrition addresses common side effects like nausea, appetite loss, and taste changes through gentle dietary adjustments.
*   **Cancer-related fatigue** is managed through a combination of balanced intake and light activity, such as regular walking, which can help increase energy.
*   Supportive care services are available 24/7 to help patients and caregivers navigate these challenges with expert guidance.

## Easy-to-Eat Foods
*   Choosing soft, nutrient-dense foods can help when appetite is low or if there is difficulty swallowing.
*   Fiber is considered an "unsung nutrient" that is vital for keeping things moving regularly, which can improve overall abdominal comfort.
*   Small, frequent portions of favorite foods are often better tolerated than large, structured meals.

## Hydration
*   Maintaining hydration is a key part of patient support, focusing on thirst relief and mouth comfort.
*   Sipping water, clear broths, or using ice chips can help manage dry mouth and keep the patient refreshed.
*   The goal of hydration in this phase is to support the patient's immediate comfort rather than purely clinical fluid balance.

## Respecting Food Preferences
*   Respecting a patient’s unique "survivor journey" means honoring their individual food choices and cultural preferences.
*   Personalized nutrition plans are created to support the patient’s dignity and sense of "normalcy".
*   Caregivers are encouraged to provide foods that the patient finds most appealing and comforting.

## Improving Quality of Life
*   Staying nourished helps patients maintain the strength needed to participate in daily activities and spend time with loved ones.
*   Access to supportive care and resources like Hope Lodge ensures that patients have a supportive space to rest and eat.
*   By focusing on comfort, nutrition helps preserve a sense of well-being and improves the overall experience of care.

## Key Facts
*   **1 in 3** men and women will be diagnosed with cancer in their lifetime.
*   There are over **18 million** cancer survivors alive in the US today who benefit from supportive care.
*   The American Cancer Society has invested over **$5.6 billion** in research since 1946 to improve patient support and quality of life.
*   Nutritional care is officially recognized as a **human right**, as established by the Vienna Declaration.
*   Regular **walking** is a clinically recognized tool for reducing cancer-related fatigue and improving energy.
