# Nutrition During Chemotherapy Care

## Introduction
*   Good nutrition serves as a foundation for cancer care, helping your body handle the physical stress of chemotherapy and maintain your strength .
*   While standard healthy eating focuses on long-term prevention, nutrition during active treatment shifts toward **maintaining weight** and helping the body tolerate intensive medical therapies .
*   Receiving proper nutritional care is officially recognized as a fundamental **human right** for every patient facing cancer.

## What is Chemotherapy?
*   Chemotherapy is a localized or systemic treatment that uses powerful drugs to destroy cancer cells .
*   It may be used to shrink tumors, prevent the spread of cancer, or treat cancer that has returned after previous therapies .
*   While these drugs target cancer, they can also affect healthy cells, leading to various side effects that impact your ability to eat and drink normally .

## Common Nutrition Challenges During Chemotherapy
*   **Nausea:** A sick feeling in the stomach that is one of the most common side effects of chemotherapy drugs .
*   **Vomiting:** The act of throwing up, which can lead to a dangerous loss of fluids and essential nutrients .
*   **Loss of Appetite:** A lack of hunger or desire to eat, often caused by the treatment itself or the metabolic impact of the cancer .
*   **Taste Changes:** Foods may taste metallic, bitter, or simply lack their usual flavour .
*   **Mouth Sores:** Painful inflammation or ulcers in the mouth and throat that make chewing and swallowing difficult .
*   **Fatigue:** Extreme exhaustion; notably, regular **walking** has been clinically shown to help reduce this specific symptom and increase energy.
*   **Diarrhea:** Frequent, loose, or watery bowel movements that can lead to rapid dehydration .
*   **Constipation:** Difficulty passing stools, often related to pain medications or decreased physical activity during treatment .

## Nutrition Goals During Chemotherapy
*   Maintain a stable body weight and preserve muscle mass to stay as strong as possible .
*   Provide the body with enough **protein and calories** to repair healthy tissues damaged by the drugs .
*   Support the immune system to reduce the risk of secondary infections during therapy .
*   Manage side effects effectively so you can continue your treatment on schedule and at the prescribed dose .

## Recommended Foods
*   **Soft Proteins:** Greek yogurt, eggs, tofu, and moist poultry or fish are excellent for tissue repair and easier to chew .
*   **Healthy Fats:** Avocados, olive oil, and nut butters provide concentrated calories to help maintain weight .
*   **Fiber-Rich Foods:** Beans, grains, and vegetables feed your **microbiome** and help keep digestion regular, provided you do not have severe diarrhea.
*   **Bland Options:** Crackers, toast, and plain rice are often better tolerated when experiencing nausea or bowel changes .

## Foods to Limit or Avoid
*   **Irritants:** Limit spicy, acidic (citrus/tomato), or very crunchy foods if you have painful mouth sores .
*   **Greasy or Fried Foods:** These can worsen nausea and are often harder for the body to digest during active treatment .
*   **Infection Risks:** Avoid raw seafood, unpasteurized dairy, or undercooked meats if your immune system is weakened by therapy .
*   **Sugary Drinks:** Limit "empty calorie" beverages that provide sugar without the nutrients needed for healing .

## Hydration During Chemotherapy
*   Staying hydrated is critical for helping your kidneys flush out treatment byproducts and chemotherapy drugs .
*   Take small, frequent sips of water, clear broths, or electrolyte drinks throughout the day .
*   Try to drink most of your fluids **between meals** rather than during them to avoid feeling too full to eat solid food .

## Meal Planning Tips
*   **Small, Frequent Meals:** Aim for 6–8 small snacks or "mini-meals" daily rather than three large ones to make eating feel less overwhelming .
*   **Eat When Hungry:** If your appetite is best in the morning, make breakfast your largest and most nutrient-dense meal .
*   **Moisten Foods:** Use extra sauces, gravies, or broths to make dry foods easier to chew and swallow if your mouth is dry .
*   **Cold Foods:** Serve foods at room temperature or cold if strong aromas trigger your nausea .

## Sample One-Day Meal Ideas
*   **Breakfast:** Soft oatmeal made with full-fat milk and topped with mashed berries .
*   **Mid-Morning Snack:** A fruit and yogurt smoothie enriched with a spoonful of almond butter .
*   **Lunch:** Creamy chicken or lentil soup served with soft crackers .
*   **Afternoon Snack:** Cottage cheese with canned peaches or half an avocado .
*   **Dinner:** Flaked salmon with mashed sweet potato and well-cooked green beans .
*   **Evening Snack:** A small bowl of pudding or a piece of soft cheese .

## When to Contact a Healthcare Provider
*   If you experience **rapid or significant unintentional weight loss** .
*   If you are unable to eat or drink for more than 24 hours .
*   If severe diarrhea or vomiting prevents you from staying hydrated .
*   If mouth pain or swallowing difficulty makes the act of eating distressing or impossible .

## Key Facts
*   **1 in 3 men and women** will receive a cancer diagnosis in their lifetime, highlighting the importance of supportive care.
*   There are over **18 million cancer survivors** currently alive in the US today who have navigated these treatment challenges.
*   **Malnutrition** is a common risk in cancer care, affecting between 20% and 80% of patients depending on the stage and type of disease .
*   The American Cancer Society has invested more than **$5.6 billion in research** since 1946 to improve patient support and survival outcomes.
*   Dietary fiber is an "unsung nutrient" that most people lack, yet it is essential for feeding the gut microbiome during care.
