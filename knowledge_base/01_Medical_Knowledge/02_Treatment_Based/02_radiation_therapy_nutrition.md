# Nutrition During Radiation Therapy

## Introduction
*   Radiation therapy requires significant energy from your body to repair healthy tissues affected during treatment.
*   Maintaining good nutrition helps you stay on your treatment schedule and improves your overall quality of life.
*   Nutritional care is recognized as a fundamental **human right** for all cancer patients.

## What is Radiation Therapy?
*   Radiation therapy is a localized treatment that uses high-energy particles or waves to destroy cancer cells.
*   Unlike systemic treatments, radiation side effects are typically specific to the area of the body being treated.

## Common Side Effects Affecting Nutrition
*   **Dry Mouth:** A decrease in saliva that makes chewing and swallowing difficult.
*   **Mouth Sores:** Painful inflammation or ulcers in the mouth and throat.
*   **Difficulty Swallowing:** Pain or a feeling of a "lump" in the throat during meals.
*   **Nausea:** A sick feeling in the stomach that can reduce your drive to eat.
*   **Fatigue:** Extreme tiredness; regular **walking** has been shown to help reduce this specific symptom.
*   **Diarrhea:** Frequent or watery stools, common if radiation is directed at the abdomen or pelvis.

## Nutrition Goals During Radiation Therapy
*   Maintain a stable weight and preserve muscle mass to stay strong.
*   Provide enough **protein and calories** for cellular repair and tissue healing.
*   Ensure adequate hydration to help the body process the effects of treatment.

## Recommended Foods
*   **Soft Proteins:** Scrambled eggs, Greek yogurt, tofu, and moist, tender meats.
*   **Healthy Fats:** Avocado, olive oil, and nut butters for concentrated energy.
*   **Cooked Grains:** Oatmeal, soft pasta, and well-cooked rice.
*   **Fiber-Rich Foods:** These feed your **microbiome**; however, intake should be adjusted based on bowel symptoms.

## Foods to Limit or Avoid
*   **Irritants (If mouth sores are present):** Limit spicy foods, acidic citrus, and very salty or crunchy foods.
*   **High-Fiber/Greasy Foods (If diarrhea is present):** Temporarily limit these until bowel movements stabilize.
*   **Infection Risks:** Avoid raw seafood, unwashed produce, or undercooked meats if your immune system is weakened.

## Hydration Tips
*   Take small, frequent sips of water throughout the day rather than drinking large amounts at once.
*   Incorporate liquid calories like smoothies, shakes, and clear broths if solid food is unappealing.
*   Limit caffeine if you are experiencing dry mouth or diarrhea.

## Eating Tips During Radiation
*   **Small, Frequent Meals:** Aim for 6–8 mini-meals daily to avoid feeling overwhelmed by large portions.
*   **Moistened Bites:** Use extra gravies, sauces, or broths to make swallowing easier and more comfortable.
*   **Temperature Control:** Serve foods at room temperature or cold if strong aromas trigger nausea.

## Sample Meal Ideas
*   **Breakfast:** Oatmeal with mashed banana and a drizzle of nut butter.
*   **Lunch:** Creamy vegetable soup with soft white bread for dipping.
*   **Dinner:** Flaked salmon with mashed sweet potato and well-cooked zucchini.
*   **Snacks:** Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, or a fruit smoothie.

## When to Contact a Healthcare Provider
*   If you experience **rapid or significant unintentional weight loss**.
*   If you are unable to drink or eat for more than 24 hours.
*   If mouth pain or swallowing difficulty prevents you from meeting your basic needs.

## Key Facts
*   **1 in 3 men and women** will face a cancer diagnosis in their lifetime.
*   There are over **18 million cancer survivors** currently alive in the US today who have managed these challenges.
*   Proper nutrition and regular activity, like walking, are vital tools for managing treatment-related fatigue.
