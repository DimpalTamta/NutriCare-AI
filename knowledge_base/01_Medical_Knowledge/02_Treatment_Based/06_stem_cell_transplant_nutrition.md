# Nutrition During Stem Cell Transplant

## Introduction
*   **Nutrition support** is a cornerstone of the stem cell transplant journey, providing the fuel your body needs to rebuild after intensive therapy.
*   Maintaining your nutritional status is recognized as a **fundamental human right** for all patients undergoing cancer treatment.
*   A well-managed diet helps you tolerate the high-dose treatments often required before a transplant.

## What is Stem Cell Transplant?
*   A stem cell transplant is a procedure to replace damaged or destroyed bone marrow with healthy stem cells.
*   It is a common treatment for blood-related cancers such as **leukemia, lymphoma, and multiple myeloma**.
*   The process often involves high-dose chemotherapy or radiation to prepare the body for the new cells.

## Why Nutrition is Important
*   **Tissue Repair:** Your body requires extra protein and calories to repair healthy cells damaged by intensive treatment.
*   **Immune Support:** Proper nutrition provides the vitamins and minerals necessary for your new immune system to grow and function.
*   **Fatigue Management:** Staying well-nourished and hydrated is essential for combatting the extreme exhaustion often felt during the transplant process.

## Low-Microbial (Neutropenic) Diet
*   Because stem cell transplants temporarily wipe out your immune system, you are at a very high risk for infections from foodborne bacteria.
*   A **low-microbial diet** focuses on strict food safety to protect you while your white blood cell counts are low.
*   The goal is to eliminate foods most likely to carry harmful bacteria, viruses, or fungi.

## Safe Food Choices
*   **Cooked Foods:** Ensure all meats, poultry, and fish are cooked to safe internal temperatures.
*   **Pasteurized Products:** Only consume pasteurized milk, cheese, yogurt, and juices.
*   **Thoroughly Washed Produce:** If allowed by your team, only eat fruits and vegetables that have been scrubbed or peeled.
*   **Packaged Items:** Individually wrapped snacks and sterilized, shelf-stable products are generally safer.

## Foods to Avoid
*   **Raw or Undercooked Proteins:** Avoid sushi, raw oysters, rare meats, and "runny" eggs.
*   **Unpasteurized Dairy:** Avoid raw milk and "soft" cheeses (like Brie or Feta) unless they are clearly labeled as pasteurized.
*   **Raw Sprouts:** Avoid alfalfa, clover, or bean sprouts, as they are high-risk for bacteria.
*   **Unwashed Produce:** Avoid any fruits or vegetables that cannot be thoroughly cleaned.

## Hydration
*   **Flushing Byproducts:** Staying hydrated helps your kidneys filter out the byproducts of intensive chemotherapy.
*   **Fluid Choices:** Focus on safe, bottled water, pasteurized juices, and clear broths.
*   **Small Sips:** If you feel nauseated, take small, frequent sips throughout the day rather than drinking large amounts at once.

## Managing Side Effects
*   **Mouth Sores:** Choose soft, moist, and bland foods; avoid acidic citrus or spicy seasonings that can cause pain.
*   **Nausea:** Eat small, frequent "mini-meals" and choose room-temperature foods that have less aroma.
*   **Fatigue:** Clinical evidence shows that **regular walking** can significantly help reduce cancer-related fatigue and improve your energy levels.

## Nutrition During Recovery
*   **Microbiome Health:** As your system recovers, increasing **dietary fiber** is vital for feeding your microbiome, which supports overall health.
*   **Protein for Strength:** Continue prioritizing high-quality protein to rebuild muscle mass lost during your hospital stay.
*   **Long-Term Habits:** Gradually transition toward a plant-forward diet rich in whole grains and colorful produce to support long-term survivorship.

## Key Facts
*   **1 in 3** men and women will be diagnosed with cancer in their lifetime.
*   There are currently more than **18 million** cancer survivors alive in the United States today.
*   The American Cancer Society has invested over **$5.6 billion** in research and patient support since 1946.
*   Dietary fiber is considered an "unsung nutrient" that most people do not get enough of, yet it is essential for gut health.
*   Nutritional care is officially recognized as a **human right** for all patients undergoing the cancer journey.
