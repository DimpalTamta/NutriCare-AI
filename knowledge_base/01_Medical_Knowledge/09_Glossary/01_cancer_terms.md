# Cancer Terms Glossary

## Overview
This glossary provides simple, clear definitions of common cancer-related terms. Understanding these words can help you better communicate with your healthcare team and make informed decisions about your care.

---

## Cancer
A term for diseases in which abnormal cells divide without control and can invade nearby tissues. Cancer cells can also spread to other parts of the body through the blood and lymph systems.

## Tumour
An abnormal mass of tissue that forms when cells grow and divide more than they should or do not die when they should. Tumours may be benign (not cancer) or malignant (cancer).

## Benign Tumour
A growth that is not cancer. Benign tumours may grow larger but do not spread to other parts of the body. They are not life-threatening but may cause problems if they press on nearby organs.

## Malignant Tumour
A cancerous tumour that can invade nearby tissues and spread to other parts of the body. Malignant tumours are also simply known as cancers.

## Neoplasm
An abnormal growth of tissue. The World Health Organization (WHO) classifies neoplasms into four main groups: benign, in situ, malignant, and of uncertain or unknown behavior.

## Metastasis
The spread of cancer from one part of the body to another. A tumour formed by cells that have spread is called a "metastatic tumour" or a "metastasis". Cancer cells break away from the original (primary) tumour, travel through the blood or lymph system, and form a new tumour in other organs or tissues.

## Oncology
The study of cancer. A branch of medicine that specializes in the diagnosis and treatment of cancer. It includes medical oncology (drugs), radiation oncology (radiation therapy), and surgical oncology (surgery).

## Chemotherapy
Treatment that uses drugs to stop the growth of cancer cells, either by killing the cells or by stopping them from dividing. It may be given by mouth, injection, or infusion.

## Radiation Therapy
The use of high-energy radiation from x-rays, gamma rays, neutrons, protons, and other sources to kill cancer cells and shrink tumours. It is often used as part of curative therapy and occasionally as a component of palliative treatment.

## Immunotherapy
Treatment to boost or restore the ability of the immune system to fight cancer, infections, and other diseases. It uses substances to stimulate or suppress the immune system to help the body fight cancer.

## Hormone Therapy
Treatment that blocks or lowers the amount of circulating hormones to help slow down or stop cancer growth. Commonly used in breast and prostate cancer.

## Targeted Therapy
Biological treatments used to target specific proteins on cancer cells that give them a competitive advantage, or block blood vessel growth, thereby inhibiting growth and survival of cancer cells. Usually used if specific mutations or proteins are present in the cancer cells.

## Surgery
A procedure to remove cancer from the body. It is usually the definitive/curative management for most cancers if appropriate. Can also be used as a palliative measure to debulk a tumour if it will improve symptoms or quality of life.

## Biopsy
The removal of cells or tissues for examination by a pathologist. The pathologist studies the tissue under a microscope or performs other tests to see if cancer cells are present.

## Diagnosis
The process of identifying a disease, condition, or injury from its signs and symptoms. A cancer diagnosis is made based on tests, procedures, and pathology results.

## Prognosis
The likely outcome or course of a disease. It is the chance of recovery or recurrence. Prognosis is based on the type and stage of cancer, as well as the patient's overall health.

## Remission
A decrease in or disappearance of signs and symptoms of cancer. In partial remission, some but not all signs and symptoms have disappeared. In complete remission, all signs and symptoms have disappeared.

## Recurrence
Cancer that has returned after a period of remission. It can come back in the same place as the original tumour (local recurrence) or in another part of the body (distant recurrence).

## Stage
A description of the size of the cancer and its spread. Staging is used to plan treatment and estimate prognosis. The TNM (tumour, node, metastasis) staging system is often used.

## Grade
A description of how abnormal cancer cells look under a microscope. The higher the grade, the more abnormal the cells look and therefore are more likely to spread.

## Screening
Checking for cancer or conditions that may lead to cancer in people who have no symptoms. Screening tests can help find cancers at an early stage, when they are easier to treat.

## Early Detection
Finding cancer at an early stage, before it has spread. Early detection increases the chances of successful treatment and cure.

## Palliative Care
Specialized medical care focused on providing relief from the symptoms and stress of a serious illness. Its goal is to improve quality of life for both the patient and the family.

## Survivorship
The phase of the cancer journey that begins after active treatment ends. It focuses on life after cancer, including managing long-term side effects, preventing recurrence, and promoting overall health and well-being.
