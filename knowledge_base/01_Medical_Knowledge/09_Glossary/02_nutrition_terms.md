# Nutrition Terms Glossary

## Overview
This glossary explains common nutrition words you may encounter in this chatbot. Understanding these terms can help you make informed choices about your diet during and after cancer treatment.

---

## Calories
A unit used to measure energy. Calories are the energy your body uses to do everyday tasks. Your calorie needs depend on your eating habits, preferences, and goals.

## Protein
A macronutrient vital for building and repairing tissues, making enzymes and hormones, and supporting immune function. Each gram of protein provides 4 calories.

## Carbohydrates
A macronutrient that is the preferred fuel for the body. Each gram of carbohydrate provides 4 calories. Carbohydrates include sugar, starch, and fiber.

## Healthy Fats
A macronutrient that is a dense source of energy and is crucial for cell structure, hormone production, and nutrient absorption. Each gram of fat provides 9 calories.

## Dietary Fiber
A type of carbohydrate that the body cannot digest. Fiber does not yield any calories. It is important for digestive health and can help manage constipation and maintain a healthy weight.

## Vitamins
Micronutrients that are essential for normal growth and function. Vitamins are needed in small quantities and must be obtained from food or supplements.

## Minerals
Micronutrients that are essential for normal body function. Examples include calcium, iron, and zinc. They are needed in small quantities and must be obtained from food or supplements.

## Hydration
The process of maintaining adequate body fluids. Proper hydration is essential for kidney function, digestion, and overall health, especially during cancer treatment.

## Electrolytes
Minerals that help balance body fluids and support nerve and muscle function. Common electrolytes include sodium, potassium, calcium, and magnesium.

## Antioxidants
Substances that may protect cells from damage caused by free radicals. They are found in many fruits, vegetables, and other plant-based foods. Some high-dose antioxidants may interfere with radiation or chemotherapy.

## Macronutrients
Nutrients needed in relatively large amounts that provide energy. They include carbohydrates, proteins, and fats.

## Micronutrients
Nutrients needed in small quantities. They include vitamins and minerals.

## Portion Size
The amount of food you choose to eat at one time. It is a practical measure for controlling calorie and nutrient intake.

## Balanced Diet
A diet that provides all nutrients in adequate amounts and proper proportions. The ICMR guidelines recommend sourcing macronutrients and micronutrients from a minimum of eight food groups.

## Malnutrition
A condition that results from eating a diet in which nutrients are either not enough or are too much such that it causes health problems. It can lead to weakness, weight loss, and poor treatment outcomes.

## BMI (Body Mass Index)
A measure of body fat based on height and weight. It is used to screen for weight categories that may lead to health problems.

## Calories per Serving
The number of calories in one serving of a food. This information is typically found on nutrition facts labels.

## Daily Value (DV)
A reference amount of nutrients to consume or not to exceed each day. It is used on nutrition labels to help you understand how a food fits into a daily diet.

## Nutrient Density
A measure of the amount of nutrients a food provides relative to the number of calories it contains. Nutrient-dense foods provide vitamins, minerals, and other beneficial substances with relatively few calories.

## Food Safety
Practices and procedures to prevent foodborne illness. This is especially important for cancer patients with weakened immune systems.
