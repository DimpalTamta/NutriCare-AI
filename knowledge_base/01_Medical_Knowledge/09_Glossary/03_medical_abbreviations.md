# Medical Abbreviations Glossary

## Overview
This glossary explains common medical abbreviations you may encounter during your cancer journey. Understanding these abbreviations can help you better understand your medical reports and conversations with your healthcare team.

---

| Abbreviation | Full Form | Meaning |
|--------------|-----------|---------|
| **ACS** | American Cancer Society | A nationwide, community-based voluntary health organization dedicated to eliminating cancer |
| **AIIMS** | All India Institute of Medical Sciences | A group of public medical colleges and hospitals in India |
| **BMI** | Body Mass Index | A measure of body fat based on height and weight. Used to screen for weight categories that may lead to health problems. |
| **BMR** | Basal Metabolic Rate | The number of calories your body needs to perform basic life-sustaining functions while at rest |
| **CBC** | Complete Blood Count | A common blood test used to check your overall health and detect disorders like anemia |
| **CT / CT Scan** | Computed Tomography | A series of X-ray images used to create detailed pictures of areas inside the body |
| **DV** | Daily Value | A reference amount of nutrients to consume or not to exceed each day. Used on nutrition labels. |
| **FDA** | Food and Drug Administration | A U.S. agency responsible for protecting public health by ensuring the safety of food and drugs |
| **GI** | Gastrointestinal | Relating to the stomach and intestines |
| **HPV** | Human Papillomavirus | A virus that can cause several types of cancer, including cervical cancer |
| **ICMR** | Indian Council of Medical Research | The apex body in India for the formulation, coordination, and promotion of biomedical research |
| **IV** | Intravenous | Medications or nutrition delivered directly into a vein |
| **MRI** | Magnetic Resonance Imaging | A scan that uses magnets and radio waves to create detailed images of organs and tissues |
| **NCI** | National Cancer Institute | The U.S. government's principal agency for cancer research and information |
| **NG Tube** | Nasogastric Tube | A tube passed through the nose into the stomach for feeding (short-term use) |
| **NIN** | National Institute of Nutrition | An Indian institute that conducts research and provides guidelines on nutrition |
| **ONS** | Oral Nutritional Supplements | High-protein or high-calorie drinks used to boost nutrition when food intake is insufficient |
| **PEG Tube** | Percutaneous Endoscopic Gastrostomy Tube | A feeding tube placed directly into the stomach through the abdominal wall (long-term use) |
| **PET** | Positron Emission Tomography | A scan that uses a radioactive tracer to show how tissues and organs are functioning |
| **PET-CT** | Positron Emission Tomography-Computed Tomography | A combination of PET and CT scans that provides both functional and anatomical information |
| **RDA** | Recommended Dietary Allowance | The daily dietary intake level that meets the requirements of almost all healthy individuals |
| **TMC** | Tata Memorial Centre | A comprehensive cancer centre in India |
| **TPN** | Total Parenteral Nutrition | Nutrition given directly into the bloodstream through a vein |
| **USDA** | United States Department of Agriculture | A U.S. agency that provides guidance on nutrition and food safety |
| **WHO** | World Health Organization | A specialized agency of the United Nations responsible for international public health |
