# Managing Dehydration During Cancer Treatment

## Introduction
*   Proper hydration is a vital part of your cancer care, helping your body function at its best.
*   Staying hydrated is essential before, during, and after your treatments to support healing.
*   **Oncology registered dietitians** are available to help you manage your fluid intake and nutrition needs.

## What is Dehydration?
*   Dehydration occurs when your body loses more fluid than you take in.
*   It can make you feel weak, tired, and put you at an increased risk for **malnutrition**.

## Common Causes
*   **Side effects of treatment:** Nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea are common causes of fluid loss.
*   **Reduced appetite:** You may not feel like eating or drinking due to changes in taste or mouth sores.
*   **Treatments:** Chemotherapy, radiation, surgery, and hormone therapy can all impact your fluid levels.

## Signs and Symptoms
*   **Information not in sources:**
    *   Feeling very thirsty or having a dry, sticky mouth.
    *   Urinating less often than usual or having dark-yellow urine.
    *   Dizziness, lightheadedness, or feeling faint.
    *   Fatigue or a deep sense of exhaustion.
    *   Cracked lips or sunken eyes.

## Nutrition Goals
*   **Maintain a healthy weight:** This is the primary goal for anyone undergoing cancer treatment.
*   **Support body function:** Fluids are essential for your organs to work correctly.
*   **Maintain energy:** Staying hydrated helps you cope with the daily challenges and fatigue of treatment.

## Best Fluids
*   **Water:** The best choice for basic hydration.
*   **High-protein shakes:** These provide both fluids and necessary nutrients if you cannot eat full meals.
*   **Information not in sources:** Broths, decaffeinated teas, and diluted fruit juices can also help keep you hydrated.

## Electrolyte Replacement
*   When you lose fluids through vomiting or diarrhea, your body also loses important minerals called electrolytes.
*   **Information not in sources:** Sports drinks or oral rehydration solutions can help replace lost sodium and potassium. Broths are also a good source of sodium.

## Foods Rich in Water
*   **Fruits and vegetables:** These are a key part of a healthy diet and have a high water content.
*   **Information not in sources:** Foods like watermelon, cucumbers, oranges, and soups can contribute significantly to your daily fluid intake.

## When to Contact a Healthcare Provider
*   If you notice **unplanned or undesirable weight loss**.
*   If side effects like **vomiting or diarrhea** prevent you from drinking enough to stay hydrated.
*   If you are too weak or tired to prepare drinks or meals for yourself.
*   If you need professional support with **tube feeding or intravenous (IV) nutrition**.
*   Ask your oncologist for a **nutrition referral** to speak with a registered dietitian.

## Frequently Asked Questions

*   **How much water should I drink?**
    *   Your needs depend on your weight and treatment side effects. An oncology dietitian can provide a customized goal.
*   **What are the best drinks for hydration?**
    *   Water is primary, but nutrient-dense options like high-protein supplements are often recommended during treatment.
*   **Can I drink sports drinks?**
    *   **Information not in sources:** Generally yes, especially if you have diarrhea or vomiting, but watch for high sugar content.
*   **What are signs of dehydration?**
    *   **Information not in sources:** Thirst, dark urine, and dizziness are the most common early warnings.
*   **When should I go to the hospital?**
    *   **Information not in sources:** Go if you cannot keep any liquids down for 24 hours, if you feel extremely confused, or if you cannot stand up without feeling faint.

## Key Facts
*   Cancer was responsible for nearly **10 million deaths** worldwide in 2024.
*   Proper nutritional care is recognized as a **human right** for all patients.
*   Approximately **38% of cancers** can be prevented through healthy lifestyle choices, including a healthy diet.
