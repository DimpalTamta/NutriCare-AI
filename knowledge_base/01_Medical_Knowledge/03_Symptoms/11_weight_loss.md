# Managing Weight Loss During Cancer Treatment

## Introduction
*   Maintaining proper nutrition is vital before, during, and after cancer treatment to help your body stay strong.
*   Weight maintenance is often the most important goal for patients undergoing active treatment.
*   Proper attention to your diet can lead to an easier recovery and help you cope with the challenges of treatment.

## What Causes Weight Loss?
*   Treatments such as chemotherapy, radiation therapy, surgery, and immunotherapy can significantly decrease your appetite.
*   These procedures and medications can drain your energy, putting you at risk for malnutrition.
*   Common side effects like nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and taste changes can make it difficult to eat enough.

## Nutrition Goals
*   **Maintain a healthy weight:** This is the primary goal to ensure you have the strength to complete your treatment.
*   **Rebuild tissues:** Eating enough protein helps your body repair tissues that cancer treatment may harm.
*   **Maintain energy:** High-calorie foods provide the fuel your body needs to heal properly.

## High-Calorie Foods
*   Dietary advice during treatment may seem like the **"opposite"** of a normal healthy diet because the focus shifts to preventing weight loss.
*   Incorporate energy-dense foods into every meal to maximize your calorie intake.
*   **Information not in sources:** Dietitians often recommend adding healthy fats like olive oil, avocado, butter, or heavy cream to meals to boost calories without adding bulk.

## High-Protein Foods
*   Protein is the building block your body needs to stay strong and repair itself.
*   Focus on a wide variety of high-protein foods like meat, fish, poultry, eggs, beans, and Greek yogurt.
*   High-protein shakes and oral supplements can be an easy way to get protein when you don't feel like eating a full meal.

## Meal Frequency
*   If your appetite is low, do not force yourself to eat three large meals.
*   Try eating **5 to 6 small meals or snacks** throughout the day instead.
*   Eat when you feel your best; for many, this is in the morning.

## Healthy Snacks
*   Keep high-calorie, high-protein snacks nearby so they are ready when you feel a spark of hunger.
*   Examples include cheese sticks, nut butters, protein bars, or trail mix.
*   **Information not in sources:** Full-fat cottage cheese or crackers with hummus are also excellent energy-boosting snacks.

## Monitoring Weight
*   Keep a close eye on your weight to catch **unplanned or undesirable** weight loss early.
*   Tracking your weight helps your care team determine if your nutrition plan needs to be adjusted.

## When to Contact a Healthcare Provider
*   If you notice unplanned weight loss or a significant drop in your energy levels.
*   If side effects like nausea or mouth sores are preventing you from eating or drinking enough.
*   If you need help managing specialized nutrition, such as tube feeding or intravenous (IV) nutrition.

## Frequently Asked Questions

*   **How can I gain weight during treatment?**
    *   Focus on a high-calorie and high-protein diet, and work with an oncology dietitian to create a plan customized to your needs.
*   **What foods are high in calories?**
    *   Foods that provide a lot of energy in small bites are best. Your dietitian can help you identify energy-dense choices that you still enjoy.
*   **Should I eat more fat?**
    *   Yes, the care team may encourage more fats and proteins than usual, especially if you are weak or underweight.
*   **How often should I eat?**
    *   Frequent small meals and snacks are generally better tolerated than large meals when you have a low appetite.
*   **When should I see a dietitian?**
    *   Ask your doctor for a **nutrition referral** as soon as you have questions about your needs or if you start losing weight.

## Key Facts
*   Cancer was responsible for nearly **10 million deaths** globally in 2024.
*   Approximately **38% of cancers** can be prevented through healthy lifestyle choices, including diet and activity.
*   Access to nutritional care is a **human right** for all patients.
