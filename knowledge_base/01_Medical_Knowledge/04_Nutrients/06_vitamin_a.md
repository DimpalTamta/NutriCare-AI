### Vitamin A: Supporting Immunity and Tissue Health
## Introduction
*   Vitamin A is a fat-soluble nutrient that is essential for maintaining your overall health, especially while your body is under the stress of cancer treatment.
*   It is one of the many important vitamins and minerals that oncology registered dietitians help patients manage to support healing.
*   A diet rich in diverse fruits and vegetables is a primary way to ensure you are getting enough of this nutrient.

## What is Vitamin A?
*   Vitamin A is an essential vitamin that the body cannot make on its own; it must be consumed through food or supplements.
*   **Information not in sources:** It exists in two main forms: preformed vitamin A (found in animal products) and provitamin A carotenoids (found in plant-based foods).

## Functions
*   **Support Body Systems:** It is vital for the body's basic functioning during and after treatment.
*   **Information not in sources:** It plays a key role in maintaining healthy vision, especially in low light.
*   **Information not in sources:** It supports the growth and repair of cells throughout the body.

## Benefits
*   **Immune Support:** Vitamin A helps maintain the body's natural defense system, which can be impacted by chemotherapy or radiation.
*   **Healthy Tissues:** It helps the body rebuild tissues that cancer treatment may harm.
*   **Information not in sources:** It helps keep the linings of the mouth, throat, and lungs healthy, which is important for preventing infections.

## Food Sources
*   **Animal-Based Sources (Information not in sources):**
    *   Dairy products (milk, cheese, butter).
    *   Eggs.
    *   Liver and fish oils.
*   **Plant-Based Sources (Carotenoids):**
    *   Orange and yellow vegetables like carrots, sweet potatoes, and squash.
    *   Dark leafy greens like spinach and kale.
    *   Orange and yellow fruits like cantaloupe and apricots.

## Deficiency
*   **Information not in sources:** A lack of Vitamin A can lead to "night blindness," making it difficult to see in the dark.
*   **Information not in sources:** It can cause very dry, bumpy skin.
*   **Information not in sources:** Chronic deficiency may lead to a higher risk of infections due to a weakened immune system.

## Excess Intake
*   **Consult Your Team:** Always talk to your healthcare team or oncology dietitian before starting high-dose supplements, as they can sometimes interfere with treatment.
*   **Information not in sources:** Because Vitamin A is fat-soluble, the body stores it. Taking too much through supplements can lead to toxicity, which may cause dizziness, nausea, or liver strain.

## Daily Requirements
*   General guidance is to eat a wide variety of colorful fruits and vegetables to meet your needs naturally.
*   Your specific needs may change if you are struggling with side effects like malabsorption or if you are recovering from surgery.
*   Your oncology registered dietitian can provide a customized plan based on your health status.

## Key Facts
*   Cancer caused nearly **10 million deaths** globally in 2024, accounting for roughly one in six deaths.
*   Approximately **38% of cancers** can be prevented through healthy lifestyle choices, including a diet rich in fruits and vegetables.
*   Low fruit and vegetable intake is a major risk factor for developing several types of cancer.
*   Nutritional care is recognized as a **human right** for all patients undergoing cancer treatment.
*   Early detection and proper nutrition assistance can significantly improve the quality of life for cancer patients.
