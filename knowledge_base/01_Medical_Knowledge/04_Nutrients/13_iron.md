### Iron: Preventing Anemia and Fatigue During Treatment

## Introduction
*   Iron is an essential mineral that your body needs to stay strong and function correctly during cancer treatment.
*   It is one of the many nutrients that oncology registered dietitians help patients monitor to improve **nutrient deficiencies**.
*   Getting enough iron is a vital part of specialized nutrition care aimed at helping you optimize your health before, during, and after treatment.

## Why Iron is Important
*   **Support Body Systems:** It is essential for the basic functioning of your body while you are healing.
*   **Energy Maintenance:** Maintaining proper iron levels helps you have the energy to cope with the physical challenges of treatment.
*   **Information not in my sources:** Iron is most known for its role in creating hemoglobin, which allows your red blood cells to carry oxygen throughout your body.
*   **Information not in my sources:** It also supports healthy brain function and a strong immune system.

## Functions
*   **Tissue Health:** Along with other vitamins and minerals, iron supports the maintenance of healthy tissues that treatment may harm.
*   **Recovery Support:** Proper attention to iron and other nutrients can assist in an **easier recovery**.
*   **Information not in my sources:** Iron acts as a key component in the production of energy from the nutrients you eat.

## Iron Deficiency
*   **Weakness:** A lack of essential minerals like iron can contribute to a general feeling of weakness.
*   **Loss of Energy:** Low iron levels can lead to a significant drop in energy, making it harder to complete daily tasks.
*   **Malnutrition Risk:** If your body cannot maintain its nutrient levels, you are at an increased risk for **malnutrition**.
*   **Information not in my sources:** Signs of deficiency may include pale skin, shortness of breath, dizziness, and feeling cold all the time.

## Iron-Rich Foods
*   **Animal Protein Sources:**
    *   Lean red meats.
    *   Poultry (chicken and turkey).
    *   Fish and seafood.
    *   Eggs.
*   **Plant-Based Sources:**
    *   Beans and lentils.
    *   **Information not in my sources:** Tofu, dark leafy greens (like spinach), and iron-fortified cereals or breads.

## Improving Iron Absorption
*   **Consult an Expert:** Work with a registered dietitian to learn the best ways to incorporate iron-rich foods into your diet.
*   **Information not in my sources:** Your body absorbs iron from animal sources (heme iron) more easily than from plant sources (non-heme iron).
*   **Information not in my sources:** To help your body absorb more iron from plant-based foods, pair them with foods high in **Vitamin C**, such as oranges, bell peppers, or strawberries.

## Daily Requirements
*   General guidance for cancer patients is to eat a **wide variety** of nutrient-dense foods to maintain a healthy weight.
*   Your specific iron needs may change depending on your treatment type, such as if you are preparing for or recovering from surgery.
*   Ask your healthcare team for a **nutrition referral** to speak with a dietitian about your personalized needs.

## Key Facts
*   Cancer was a leading cause of death globally in 2024, accounting for nearly **10 million deaths**.
*   Approximately **38% of cancers** can currently be prevented through healthy lifestyle choices, including a healthy diet.
*   Nutritional care, including managing mineral levels, is recognized as a **human right** for all patients.
*   Low fruit and vegetable intake is a major modifiable risk factor for many types of cancer.
*   Early detection and proper nutritional support can significantly improve a patient's **quality of life**.
