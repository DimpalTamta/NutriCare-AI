### Potassium: Fluid Balance and Heart Health

## Introduction
*   Potassium is an essential mineral and electrolyte that your body needs to function properly every day.
*   For cancer patients, maintaining the right balance of minerals is part of specialized nutrition care to support the body during and after treatment.
*   Oncology registered dietitians help monitor your levels to address any nutrient deficiencies that may arise during your care.

## Why Potassium is Important
*   **Body Functioning:** Potassium is vital for the basic operation of your cells and organs.
*   **Energy and Strength:** Maintaining proper mineral levels helps you stay strong and provides the energy needed to cope with treatment challenges.
*   **Fluid Balance:** **Information not in sources:** As an electrolyte, it helps your body maintain the right amount of fluid inside your cells.
*   **Recovery Support:** Proper attention to nutrition, including minerals, can lead to an easier recovery from surgery or treatment.

## Functions
*   **Muscle and Nerve Health:** **Information not in sources:** Potassium is necessary for your muscles to contract and your nerves to send signals throughout your body.
*   **Heart Health:** **Information not in sources:** It plays a critical role in keeping your heartbeat regular.
*   **Supporting Tissues:** Along with protein and other nutrients, it helps maintain healthy tissues that treatment may harm.

## Food Sources
*   **Fruits and Vegetables:** Eating a wide variety of plant-based foods is a primary way to get potassium.
    *   **Information not in sources:** Bananas, oranges, melons, and apricots.
    *   **Information not in sources:** Potatoes, sweet potatoes, spinach, and mushrooms.
*   **Dairy Products:** Milk and yogurt [Information not in sources].
*   **Legumes:** **Information not in sources:** Beans, lentils, and peas.
*   **Animal Proteins:** Meat and fish [Information not in sources].

## Deficiency
*   **Side Effect Impact:** Treatments that cause vomiting or diarrhea can lead to a loss of electrolytes like potassium.
*   **Malnutrition Risk:** An inability to maintain proper nutrient levels puts you at a higher risk for malnutrition.
*   **Weakness:** A lack of essential minerals can contribute to feeling weak, tired, or having low energy.
*   **Information not in sources:** Low potassium (hypokalemia) can cause muscle cramps, constipation, or an irregular heartbeat.

## Daily Requirements
*   The general goal is to eat a wide variety of nutrient-dense, high-calorie, and high-protein foods to support your body's needs.
*   Your requirements may change if you are experiencing severe side effects or are recovering from surgery.
*   If you cannot meet your needs through food alone, your care team may suggest oral supplements or specialized nutrition support.
*   Ask your doctor for a **nutrition referral** to speak with a registered dietitian for a personalized plan based on your medical history.

## Key Facts
*   Cancer was a leading cause of death globally in 2024, accounting for nearly **10 million deaths**.
*   Approximately **38% of cancers** can be prevented through healthy lifestyle factors, including a healthy diet.
*   Nutritional care is recognized as a **human right** for all patients to support their health and dignity.
*   Low fruit and vegetable intake is a major modifiable risk factor that contributes to the global cancer burden.
*   The primary goal of nutrition during active treatment is to **maintain a healthy weight**.
