###  Vitamin C: Tissue Repair and Immune Support


## Introduction
*   Vitamin C is a water-soluble nutrient that is essential for your body to function properly during cancer treatment.
*   Because your body cannot produce its own Vitamin C, you must get it through the foods you eat.
*   Oncology registered dietitians help patients ensure they are getting enough of this nutrient to support their recovery.

## Why It Matters / Functions
*   **Tissue Repair:** Vitamin C is vital for helping the body rebuild and repair tissues that may be harmed by chemotherapy, radiation, or surgery.
*   **Immune Support:** It helps maintain a healthy immune system, which is important for protecting your body during treatment.
*   **Energy Maintenance:** Getting proper nutrients like Vitamin C helps you maintain the energy needed to cope with treatment challenges.
*   **General Health:** It is essential for the basic functioning of all your body's systems.

## Food Sources
*   **Fruits:** Such as oranges, grapefruit, strawberries, kiwi, and cantaloupe.
*   **Vegetables:** Including bell peppers, broccoli, tomatoes, and leafy greens.
*   **General Guidance:** Eating a "wide variety" of colorful fruits and vegetables is the best way to get enough Vitamin C naturally.

## Deficiency
*   **Slow Healing:** A lack of Vitamin C can make it harder for your body to heal properly after surgery or treatment.
*   **Weakness:** Not getting enough nutrients can lead to feeling tired or weak.
*   **Increased Risk:** Poor intake of fruits and vegetables is a known risk factor that can contribute to a higher cancer burden.

## Supplement Considerations
*   **Ask First:** Always talk to your oncologist or oncology dietitian before taking Vitamin C supplements.
*   **High Doses:** Very high doses of antioxidants like Vitamin C can sometimes interfere with how certain cancer treatments work.
*   **Food First:** It is generally recommended to get your vitamins from whole foods whenever possible.

## Daily Requirements
*   General guidance is to include a serving of fruit or vegetables with every meal to meet your needs.
*   Your specific needs may increase if you are recovering from surgery or dealing with severe side effects.
*   An oncology dietitian can provide a customized nutrition plan to ensure your levels are optimized.

## Key Facts
*   Cancer was a leading cause of death globally in 2024, accounting for nearly **10 million deaths**.
*   Approximately **38% of cancers are preventable** through healthy lifestyle choices, including a diet rich in fruits and vegetables.
*   Nutritional care is recognized as a **human right** for every patient.
*   Low fruit and vegetable intake is one of the primary modifiable risk factors for cancer worldwide.
*   Proper nutrition during treatment can lead to an **easier recovery** and improved quality of life.
