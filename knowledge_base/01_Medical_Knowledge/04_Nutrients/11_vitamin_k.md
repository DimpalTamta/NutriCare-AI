### Vitamin K: Blood Clotting and Bone Health

## Introduction
*   Vitamin K is an essential nutrient that helps your body function properly during and after cancer treatment.
*   Staying well-nourished with essential vitamins helps you maintain the strength and energy needed to cope with treatment challenges.
*   Oncology registered dietitians help patients manage their nutrient intake to support healing and recovery.

## What is Vitamin K?
*   Vitamin K is a fat-soluble vitamin found in certain foods and produced by bacteria in your gut.
*   **Information not in sources:** It primarily exists in two forms: Vitamin K1 (found in plants) and Vitamin K2 (found in animal products and fermented foods).

## Functions
*   **Support Body Systems:** It is vital for the basic functioning of your body's systems.
*   **Tissue Health:** Along with other vitamins and minerals, it supports the body in maintaining healthy tissues.
*   **Information not in sources:** Its most well-known role is helping the body produce the proteins needed for blood clotting.
*   **Information not in sources:** It also plays a role in bone metabolism and heart health.

## Benefits
*   **Recovery Support:** Proper nutrition, including Vitamin K, can lead to an easier recovery from surgery and other treatments.
*   **Wound Healing:** **Information not in sources:** By helping your blood clot properly, Vitamin K is essential for healing surgical sites and preventing excessive bleeding.
*   **Bone Strength:** **Information not in sources:** It helps the body use calcium effectively to maintain strong bones during treatment.

## Food Sources
*   **Information not in sources:**
    *   **Green Leafy Vegetables:** Spinach, kale, collard greens, and Swiss chard are the richest sources.
    *   **Other Vegetables:** Broccoli, Brussels sprouts, and cabbage.
    *   **Fruit:** Some fruits, such as kiwi and blueberries, contain smaller amounts.
    *   **Animal Products:** Eggs, meat, and dairy products contain Vitamin K2.
    *   **Vegetable Oils:** Soybean and canola oils.

## Deficiency
*   **Malnutrition Risk:** Not getting enough essential nutrients puts you at an increased risk for malnutrition during cancer treatment.
*   **Weakness:** A lack of necessary vitamins can contribute to feeling tired or weak.
*   **Information not in sources:** The primary sign of deficiency is easy bruising or excessive bleeding from small cuts, as the blood takes longer to clot.

## Daily Requirements
*   General guidance for cancer patients focuses on eating a **wide variety of nutrient-dense foods** to support weight maintenance.
*   Your specific needs may change if you are taking certain medications, such as blood thinners (e.g., warfarin), which require consistent Vitamin K intake.
*   Consult your **oncology registered dietitian** to determine the best intake for your specific treatment and medical history.

## Key Facts
*   Cancer was responsible for nearly **10 million deaths** globally in 2024, or roughly one in six deaths.
*   Approximately **38% of cancers are preventable** through healthy lifestyle choices, including a diet rich in fruits and vegetables.
*   Nutritional care is recognized as a **human right** for all patients to support their health and dignity.
*   Low fruit and vegetable intake is a modifiable risk factor that contributes significantly to the global cancer burden.
*   Early detection and proper nutritional support can significantly improve a patient's **quality of life**.
