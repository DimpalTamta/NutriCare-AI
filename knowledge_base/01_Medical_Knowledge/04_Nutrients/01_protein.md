### Protein: An Essential Nutrient for Cancer Patients

## Introduction
*   Protein is a vital nutrient that acts as a building block for your body's cells.
*   For people with cancer, getting enough protein is essential to help the body stay strong and recover from the effects of treatment,.
*   It is a key part of specialized nutrition care provided by oncology registered dietitians.

## Why Protein is Important for Cancer Patients
*   **Tissue Repair:** Protein is needed to help rebuild and repair body tissues that may be harmed by chemotherapy, radiation, or surgery,.
*   **Muscle Maintenance:** It helps you maintain your strength and muscle mass during a time when you might feel weak,.
*   **Immune Support:** Proper protein intake supports your body’s ability to protect itself and heal properly.
*   **Preventing Malnutrition:** Eating high-protein foods helps reduce the risk of malnutrition and weight loss during treatment,.

## Daily Protein Requirements
*   Your protein needs are often higher during cancer treatment than they are during normal times.
*   Your care team may encourage a high-protein diet even if it feels different from your usual eating habits.
*   Needs can change depending on your specific treatment, such as before or after surgery.
*   Always consult your oncology registered dietitian for a plan tailored to your individual needs.

## Best Animal Protein Sources
*   Eggs,.
*   Poultry (such as chicken and turkey).
*   Fish.
*   Lean meats.
*   Dairy products like milk, yogurt, and cheese.
*   Greek yogurt (often higher in protein than regular yogurt).

## Best Plant Protein Sources
*   Beans and lentils.
*   Tofu and tempeh.
*   Nuts and nut butters.
*   Seeds.
*   Quinoa (a high-protein grain).

## Signs of Protein Deficiency
*   **Muscle Loss:** Feeling a loss of strength or noticing your muscles look smaller.
*   **Weakness:** A general sense of feeling drained or having low energy.
*   **Slow Wound Healing:** Cuts or surgical sites taking longer than expected to heal,.
*   **Fatigue:** A deep sense of exhaustion that interferes with daily life,.
*   **Information not in sources:** Clinical signs like edema (swelling) can also indicate low protein levels.

## Can Too Much Protein Be Harmful?
*   While protein is essential, very high amounts can sometimes put a strain on your kidneys.
*   It is important to balance protein with other nutrients and fluids.
*   Always talk to your healthcare team before making major changes to your intake or using supplements.

## Tips to Increase Protein Intake
*   Add eggs to your meals or snacks.
*   Choose Greek yogurt as a high-protein snack.
*   Use high-protein shakes or oral supplements between meals,.
*   Add nut butters to fruit or toast.
*   Snack on nuts and seeds throughout the day.
*   Eat your high-protein foods first when your appetite is highest, often in the morning,.

## Key Facts
*   Cancer caused nearly **10 million deaths** globally in 2024, or about one in six deaths,.
*   Nutritional care is a **human right** for all patients to support their health and dignity.
*   Up to **38% of cancers** can be prevented through healthy lifestyle choices, including a diet rich in plant proteins.
*   Early nutritional intervention can help patients cope with treatment challenges and improve quality of life,.
*   Oncology dietitians provide **customized support** to help patients stop undesirable weight loss through protein-rich plans,.

## Food Sources
*   **Animal-Based:** Lean meat, poultry, fish, eggs, milk, cheese, and yogurt.
*   **Plant-Based:** Beans, lentils, nuts, seeds, tofu, and quinoa.
*   **Supplements:** High-protein shakes and oral nutritional supplements,.
