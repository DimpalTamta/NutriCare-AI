### Healthy Fats: Concentrated Energy for Cancer Patients

## Introduction
*   Fats are a concentrated source of energy for your body.
*   In a cancer diet, fats are especially important for individuals who are struggling to maintain their weight or have a poor appetite.
*   Choosing the right types of fats can help you stay strong and provide the fuel your body needs during treatment.

## What are Fats?
*   Fats are nutrients that provide more than twice the energy of carbohydrates or protein in the same amount of food.
*   They are essential for absorbing certain vitamins and protecting your body's organs.

## Why Healthy Fats are Important
*   **Weight Maintenance:** Fats are an easy way to add calories to your diet to help stop undesirable weight loss.
*   **Energy Density:** Because they are high in calories, they allow you to get the energy you need even if you can only eat small amounts of food.
*   **Tissue Repair:** Along with protein, healthy fats support the body in rebuilding tissues that may be harmed by cancer treatments.
*   **Coping with Treatment:** Having enough energy helps you manage the daily challenges and fatigue brought on by chemotherapy or radiation.

## Types of Fats

### Unsaturated Fats
*   Often called "healthy fats," these are usually liquid at room temperature.
*   They are found primarily in plant-based foods and oils, as well as some fish.
*   They are a key part of a heart-healthy and cancer-prevention diet.

### Saturated Fats
*   These are usually solid at room temperature and are mostly found in animal products.
*   While often limited in a standard healthy diet, your care team may encourage high-calorie dairy or meats if you are underweight or weak.

### Trans Fats
*   These are processed fats often found in "fast foods" and some packaged snacks.
*   Healthy lifestyle guidelines suggest avoiding or limiting these whenever possible.

## Healthy Fat Sources
*   **Avocados:** A nutrient-dense and easy-to-eat source of healthy fat.
*   **Nuts and Nut Butters:** Such as almonds, walnuts, peanut butter, and almond butter.
*   **Seeds:** Including chia, flax, and sunflower seeds.
*   **Plant-Based Oils:** Like olive oil and canola oil.
*   **Fatty Fish:** Such as salmon or mackerel, which provide essential fatty acids.

## Foods to Limit
*   **Highly Processed Snacks:** These may contain unhealthy trans fats and provide little nutritional value.
*   **Fried Fast Foods:** Often high in unhealthy fats that can be hard to digest if you are experiencing nausea or stomach issues.

## Daily Requirements
*   There is no single "correct" amount of fat for every patient; your needs depend on your weight goals and treatment plan.
*   If you are losing weight, your oncology dietitian may suggest adding extra fats (like oils or butter) to your meals to boost calorie intake.
*   Consult your registered dietitian for a personalized plan based on your specific side effects and health status.

## Key Facts
*   Cancer accounted for nearly **10 million deaths** globally in 2024, making it a leading cause of death worldwide.
*   Approximately **38% of cancers** can be prevented by avoiding risk factors and maintaining a healthy body weight.
*   Proper nutritional care is recognized as a **human right** for all patients.
*   An unhealthy diet is a major modifiable risk factor for many types of cancer.
*   Early nutritional support can help patients tolerate treatment better and improve their overall quality of life.

## Food Sources
*   **Plant-Based:** Avocados, nuts, seeds, and olives.
*   **Oils:** Olive, canola, and vegetable oils.
*   **Animal-Based:** Eggs, dairy (cheese, whole milk, yogurt), and fatty fish.
