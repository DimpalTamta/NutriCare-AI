###  Vitamin E: Cellular Protection and Antioxidant Support

## Introduction
*   Vitamin E is an essential nutrient that helps your body function properly and protects your cells from damage.
*   For cancer patients, maintaining adequate vitamin levels is part of a plan to optimize health before, during, and after treatment.
*   An oncology registered dietitian can help you include Vitamin E-rich foods to support your recovery and strength.

## Why It Matters / Functions
*   **Support Body Systems:** Vitamin E is necessary for the basic functioning of your body’s systems during treatment.
*   **Tissue Repair:** It works alongside other nutrients to help your body rebuild and repair tissues that may be harmed by chemotherapy, radiation, or surgery.
*   **Energy Maintenance:** Proper nutrition helps you maintain the energy needed to cope with the daily challenges of cancer care.
*   **Information not in sources:** Vitamin E acts as an antioxidant, meaning it helps protect your cells from the stress and damage that can occur during illness.

## Food Sources
*   **Information not in sources:**
    *   **Nuts and Seeds:** Almonds, sunflower seeds, and hazelnuts.
    *   **Vegetable Oils:** Sunflower, safflower, and wheat germ oils.
    *   **Green Vegetables:** Spinach and broccoli.
    *   **Fortified Foods:** Some breakfast cereals and fruit juices.
*   **Practical Tip:** Many Vitamin E sources, like nuts and oils, are also **high-calorie foods**, which help you maintain a healthy weight during treatment.

## Daily Requirements
*   General guidance is to eat a wide variety of nutrient-dense foods to ensure you get enough vitamins and minerals.
*   Your specific needs may increase if you are recovering from surgery or have difficulty absorbing nutrients.
*   **Oncology dietitians** can provide customized counseling to help you meet your daily requirements based on your specific cancer type and treatment plan.

## Key Facts
*   Cancer was responsible for nearly **10 million deaths** globally in 2024, accounting for roughly one in six deaths.
*   Approximately **38% of cancers** are currently preventable through healthy lifestyle choices, including a diet rich in fruits and vegetables.
*   Nutritional care is recognized as a **human right** for all patients to support their dignity and health.
*   The primary goal of nutrition during treatment is to **maintain a healthy weight** and prevent malnutrition.
*   Low fruit and vegetable intake is one of the leading modifiable risk factors for cancer worldwide.
