### Vitamin B Complex: Energy and Nerve Health Support

## Introduction
*   Vitamin B complex is a group of essential nutrients that your body needs to function, especially during the physical stress of cancer treatment.
*   These vitamins help your body process the energy from the food you eat, which is vital for maintaining your strength.
*   Oncology registered dietitians can help you identify if you have any nutrient deficiencies and create a plan to improve them.

## What is Vitamin B Complex?
*   **Information not in sources:** Vitamin B complex is made up of eight different water-soluble vitamins that work together to support various body processes.
*   Unlike fat-soluble vitamins, these are not stored in large amounts in the body and need to be replaced regularly through your diet.

## Types of B Vitamins
*   **Information not in sources:** The complex includes B1 (thiamine), B2 (riboflavin), B3 (niacin), B5 (pantothenic acid), B6 (pyridoxine), B7 (biotin), B9 (folate/folic acid), and B12 (cobalamin).

## Functions
*   **Energy Production:** These vitamins help convert the carbohydrates, fats, and proteins you eat into the energy your body uses for daily activities and healing.
*   **Tissue Repair:** They assist in rebuilding tissues that may be harmed by chemotherapy, radiation, or surgery.
*   **Information not in sources:** They play a key role in brain function, nerve health, and the creation of red blood cells.

## Benefits
*   **Managing Fatigue:** Getting enough B vitamins may help reduce cancer-related fatigue, which is a common side effect of treatment.
*   **Immune Support:** Supports the body's natural defenses during recovery.
*   **Neurological Health:** **Information not in sources:** Certain B vitamins, like B6 and B12, are particularly important for maintaining healthy nerve cells.

## Food Sources
*   **Animal-Based:**
    *   Meat (beef, pork).
    *   Poultry (chicken, turkey).
    *   Fish and seafood.
    *   Eggs.
    *   Dairy products like milk, cheese, and yogurt.
*   **Plant-Based:**
    *   **Information not in sources:** Leafy green vegetables (spinach, kale).
    *   **Information not in sources:** Legumes (beans, lentils, chickpeas).
    *   **Information not in sources:** Fortified cereals and whole grains.
    *   **Information not in sources:** Seeds and nuts.

## Deficiency
*   **Symptoms of Deficiency (Information not in sources):**
    *   Extreme fatigue or weakness.
    *   Anemia (low red blood cell count).
    *   Confusion or memory problems.
    *   Skin rashes or cracks around the mouth.
*   Cancer treatments can sometimes cause your body to lose these nutrients more quickly or make it harder for you to absorb them.

## Daily Requirements
*   Your specific needs for B vitamins can increase during treatment to support healing and energy maintenance.
*   A varied diet including high-protein and high-calorie foods is generally recommended to meet these needs.
*   Always consult your oncology registered dietitian before taking B-vitamin supplements, as high doses can sometimes interfere with certain treatments.

## Key Facts
*   Cancer accounted for nearly **10 million deaths** globally in 2024, or roughly one in six deaths.
*   Approximately **38% of cancers** can be prevented through healthy lifestyle choices, which include a diet rich in fruits and vegetables.
*   Nutritional care is recognized as a **human right** for all patients, helping to support their dignity and health during treatment.
*   Low fruit and vegetable intake is a modifiable risk factor that contributes to the global cancer burden.
*   Early nutritional intervention can lead to an **easier recovery** and better quality of life for cancer patients.
