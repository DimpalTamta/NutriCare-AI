### Carbohydrates: Fuel for Energy During Cancer Treatment

## Introduction
*   Carbohydrates are the primary source of energy for your body.
*   For cancer patients, they provide the necessary fuel to keep your body functioning during treatment.
*   An oncology dietitian can help you choose the right types of carbohydrates to manage your energy levels and weight.

## What are Carbohydrates?
*   Carbohydrates are a type of macronutrient found in many of the foods we eat.
*   They are broken down by the body into glucose (sugar), which is the main energy source for your cells.

## Why Carbohydrates are Important
*   **Energy Levels:** They provide the energy needed to cope with the daily challenges and fatigue of cancer treatment.
*   **Weight Maintenance:** Eating enough carbohydrates helps you maintain a healthy weight, which is a primary goal during treatment.
*   **Brain Function:** Your brain relies heavily on carbohydrates to stay alert and focused.
*   **Sparing Protein:** By providing energy, carbohydrates allow the protein you eat to be used for its main job: repairing tissues damaged by treatment.

## Types of Carbohydrates

### Simple Carbohydrates
*   These are broken down quickly by the body for a fast burst of energy.
*   They are found naturally in fruits and dairy products.
*   They are also found in processed sugars and sweets.

### Complex Carbohydrates
*   These take longer to digest, providing a steady and long-lasting source of energy.
*   They are often high in fiber, which can help with digestion issues related to treatment.

## Healthy Carbohydrate Sources
*   **Fruits:** Such as apples, bananas, and berries.
*   **Vegetables:** Including starchy options like potatoes, corn, and peas.
*   **Whole Grains:** Such as oats, brown rice, and whole-wheat bread or pasta.
*   **Legumes:** Like beans, lentils, and chickpeas.

## Foods to Limit
*   While high-calorie foods are often encouraged during treatment to prevent weight loss, it is still helpful to limit certain items if they don't provide other nutrients.
*   **Sugar-sweetened beverages:** Like sodas and heavily sweetened teas.
*   **Highly processed snacks:** Which may be low in vitamins and minerals.

## Daily Requirements
*   Your specific carbohydrate needs depend on your treatment plan and energy levels.
*   The main focus is ensuring you eat enough "high-calorie" foods to stay at a healthy weight.
*   If you are having trouble eating, your care team may recommend high-calorie liquid supplements.

## Key Facts
*   Cancer was a leading cause of death in 2024, accounting for nearly **10 million deaths**.
*   Nutritional care is recognized as a **human right** for all patients.
*   Maintaining a healthy diet and body weight is a key part of reducing the cancer burden.
*   Approximately **38% of cancers** can be prevented through healthy lifestyle factors, including a diet rich in fruits and vegetables.
*   Early nutritional support can help you heal properly and make your recovery easier.

## Food Sources
*   **Whole Foods:** Fruits, vegetables, and legumes.
*   **Grains:** Bread, rice, pasta, and cereals.
*   **Energy Boosters:** High-calorie snacks and oral supplements if recommended by your dietitian.
