### Fiber: Essential for Digestive Health During Cancer Care

## Introduction
*   Fiber is an essential part of a healthy diet, especially for those undergoing cancer treatment.
*   It plays a key role in keeping your digestive system working correctly.
*   Working with an oncology dietitian helps you balance fiber to manage side effects like constipation or diarrhea.

## What is Fiber?
*   Fiber is a type of carbohydrate found in plant-based foods.
*   Unlike other nutrients, your body cannot fully digest or absorb it.
*   It passes through your digestive system mostly intact, helping to move food and waste along.

## Benefits of Fiber
*   **Cancer Prevention:** A diet rich in fruits and vegetables (which are high in fiber) reduces the risk of several cancers, including colon and rectum cancer.
*   **Digestive Health:** It helps prevent and manage constipation, a common treatment side effect.
*   **Weight Management:** Fiber-rich foods help you feel full, making it easier to maintain a healthy body weight, which is a major goal during treatment.
*   **Blood Sugar Control:** It can help regulate how your body uses sugar.

## Types of Fiber

### Soluble Fiber
*   This type dissolves in water to form a gel-like material.
*   It can help lower blood cholesterol and glucose levels.
*   It is often used to help manage diarrhea by adding bulk to stools.

### Insoluble Fiber
*   This type does not dissolve in water.
*   It adds bulk to the stool and helps food pass more quickly through the stomach and intestines.
*   It is especially helpful for those dealing with constipation.

## Foods Rich in Fiber
*   **Fruits:** Such as berries, apples, and pears.
*   **Vegetables:** Including leafy greens, broccoli, and carrots.
*   **Whole Grains:** Such as oats, brown rice, and whole-wheat bread.
*   **Legumes:** Like beans, lentils, and chickpeas.
*   **Nuts and Seeds:** Such as almonds, chia seeds, and flaxseeds.

## When Fiber Intake Should Be Modified
*   **During Diarrhea:** Your care team may suggest a **low-fiber diet** to rest your bowels and slow down digestion.
*   **During Constipation:** Increasing fiber and fluids can help move waste through your system more easily.
*   **After Surgery:** If you've had surgery on your digestive tract, your dietitian may recommend specific fiber textures to help you heal properly.
*   **Appetite Changes:** If you are struggling to eat enough calories, you might need to limit high-fiber foods that make you feel full too quickly.

## Daily Requirements
*   Most people need more fiber than they currently get in their daily diet.
*   Oncology dietitians provide personalized guidance on how much fiber you need based on your symptoms and treatment type.
*   If you increase your fiber intake, it is vital to also increase your fluid intake to prevent gas and bloating.

## Key Facts
*   Cancer caused nearly **10 million deaths** globally in 2024.
*   Low fruit and vegetable intake is a major risk factor for cancer.
*   Approximately **38% of cancers are preventable** through healthy lifestyle choices, including a high-fiber diet.
*   Nutritional care is recognized by ESPEN as a **human right** for all patients.
*   Dietitians provide 1:1 counseling to help manage treatment-related side effects.

## Food Sources
*   **Plant-Based:** Fruits, vegetables, legumes, and nuts.
*   **Grains:** Oats, barley, brown rice, and whole-grain cereals.
*   **Modified Textures:** Pureed or soft-cooked vegetables if swallowing is difficult.
