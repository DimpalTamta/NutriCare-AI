###  Magnesium: Muscle and Nerve Function Support

## Introduction
*   Magnesium is an essential mineral that helps your body perform hundreds of important tasks every day.
*   For those undergoing cancer treatment, maintaining proper levels of minerals like magnesium is a key part of staying strong.
*   Oncology registered dietitians monitor your mineral intake to help improve nutrient deficiencies and support your recovery.

## Why Magnesium is Important
*   **Body Functioning:** Magnesium is essential for the basic functioning of almost every system in your body.
*   **Energy Maintenance:** It helps you maintain the energy needed to cope with the challenges of cancer treatment, such as chemotherapy or surgery.
*   **Recovery Support:** Proper attention to mineral intake can assist in an easier recovery and help you heal properly.
*   **Healthy Weight:** Magnesium is found in many nutrient-dense foods that help you maintain a healthy weight during treatment.

## Functions
*   **Support Body Systems:** Magnesium supports the healthy operation of your heart, muscles, and nerves.
*   **Tissue Health:** It works alongside protein and vitamins to help rebuild tissues that may be harmed by cancer treatments.
*   **Information not in sources:** It plays a role in regulating blood sugar levels and blood pressure.
*   **Information not in sources:** It is vital for the structural development of bones and the transport of other minerals like calcium and potassium.

## Food Sources
*   **Nuts and Seeds:** Almonds, cashews, peanuts, and pumpkin seeds are excellent high-calorie options.
*   **Legumes:** Beans (such as black beans and edamame) and lentils.
*   **Whole Grains:** Whole-wheat bread, brown rice, and quinoa.
*   **Vegetables:** Dark leafy greens like spinach and Swiss chard.
*   **Other Sources:** **Information not in sources:** Dark chocolate, avocados, and certain types of fatty fish.

## Deficiency
*   **Weakness:** A lack of essential minerals like magnesium can contribute to feeling weak or having a sudden drop in energy.
*   **Malnutrition Risk:** If you cannot eat enough mineral-rich foods, you may be at an increased risk for malnutrition.
*   **Information not in sources:** Early signs of low magnesium can include loss of appetite, nausea, vomiting, and fatigue.
*   **Information not in sources:** Severe deficiency may lead to muscle cramps, numbness, tingling, or abnormal heart rhythms.

## Daily Requirements
*   Your specific needs for magnesium depend on your age, gender, and the specific side effects you are experiencing during treatment.
*   The general goal is to eat a wide variety of nutrient-dense, high-calorie, and high-protein foods to meet your needs naturally.
*   If you are unable to eat enough, your care team may suggest specialized nutrition support, such as oral supplements or intravenous (IV) nutrition.
*   Ask your doctor for a **nutrition referral** to speak with a registered dietitian for a personalized plan.

## Key Facts
*   Cancer was a leading cause of death globally in 2024, accounting for nearly **10 million deaths**.
*   Approximately **38% of cancers** are currently preventable through healthy lifestyle choices, including a healthy diet.
*   Nutritional care is recognized as a **human right** for all patients to support their health and dignity.
*   The primary goal of nutrition during treatment is to **maintain a healthy weight**.
*   Low fruit and vegetable intake is a major modifiable risk factor for many types of cancer.
