### Vitamin D: Bone Health and Immune Function

## Introduction
*   Vitamin D is a nutrient that helps your body function properly and stay strong throughout cancer treatment.
*   It is one of the essential vitamins that oncology registered dietitians monitor to help improve nutrient deficiencies.
*   Proper attention to nutrition, including Vitamin D, can assist in an easier recovery and help you cope with treatment challenges.

## What is Vitamin D?
*   Vitamin D is a fat-soluble vitamin that your body needs for various processes.
*   **Information not in sources:** Unlike many other vitamins, your body can produce its own Vitamin D when your skin is exposed to sunlight.

## Functions
*   **Support Body Systems:** It is essential for the body's basic functioning.
*   **Tissue Health:** Along with other nutrients, it supports the body in maintaining healthy tissues.
*   **Information not in sources:** It plays a critical role in helping your body absorb calcium to maintain strong bones.

## Benefits
*   **Recovery Support:** Maintaining adequate vitamin levels helps you heal properly during and after treatment.
*   **Energy Maintenance:** Proper nutrition helps you maintain the energy needed to handle the stress of procedures and medications.
*   **Information not in sources:** It may help support a healthy immune system, which is vital during cancer care.

## Food Sources
*   **Animal-Based Sources (Information not in sources):**
    *   Fatty fish (such as salmon, mackerel, and sardines).
    *   Egg yolks.
    *   Beef liver.
*   **Fortified Foods (Information not in sources):**
    *   Fortified milk and dairy alternatives.
    *   Fortified orange juice.
    *   Fortified cereals.

## Sunlight and Vitamin D
*   **Information not in sources:** Sunlight is a primary source of Vitamin D, as your skin makes it when exposed to UV rays.
*   **Safety Note:** While sunlight is helpful, you should always follow your care team's advice regarding sun safety and UV radiation exposure, which is a known risk factor for skin cancer.

## Deficiency
*   **Malnutrition Risk:** An inability to maintain proper vitamin levels can put you at an increased risk for malnutrition.
*   **Weakness:** A lack of essential nutrients can contribute to feeling weak or having low energy.
*   **Information not in sources:** Long-term deficiency can lead to bone pain, muscle weakness, and an increased risk of bone fractures.

## Supplement Considerations
*   **Ask Your Doctor:** Before taking any Vitamin D supplements, always ask your oncology team for a referral to a dietitian.
*   **Customized Care:** Supplements should be part of a customized and scientifically-supported nutrition plan.
*   **Information not in sources:** Because it is a fat-soluble vitamin, taking very high doses can lead to a buildup in the body, which may be harmful.

## Daily Requirements
*   Your specific needs for Vitamin D depend on your health status, treatment type, and current blood levels.
*   Oncology registered dietitians provide 1:1 counseling to help determine the right amount for you.
*   General guidance focuses on eating a wide variety of nutrient-dense foods to support weight maintenance.

## Key Facts
*   Cancer accounted for nearly **10 million deaths** globally in 2024, or roughly one in six deaths.
*   Approximately **38% of cancers** are currently preventable by avoiding risk factors and eating a healthy diet.
*   Nutritional care is recognized as a **human right** for every patient to support their dignity and health.
*   Maintaining a **healthy weight** is the most important goal for individuals undergoing cancer treatment.
*   Early detection and proper treatment give many cancers a **high chance of being cured**.
