### Calcium: Building Strong Bones During Cancer Care

## Introduction
*   Calcium is an essential mineral that your body needs to function correctly, especially during the physical stress of cancer treatment.
*   Oncology registered dietitians help patients improve nutrient deficiencies, including minerals like calcium, to support their overall health.
*   Maintaining proper nutrition is vital before, during, and after your treatment journey.

## Why Calcium is Important
*   It is a key nutrient required for your body's basic daily functioning.
*   Proper levels help you stay strong and maintain the energy needed to cope with treatment challenges.
*   **Information not in sources:** Calcium is the primary mineral used to build and maintain strong bones and teeth.
*   **Information not in sources:** It also plays a vital role in helping your muscles move and your nerves carry messages between your brain and body.

## Functions
*   **Support Body Systems:** Calcium is essential for the healthy operation of various body systems while you are healing.
*   **Tissue Health:** Along with protein and other vitamins, it supports the maintenance of healthy tissues that treatment may harm.
*   **Information not in sources:** It helps your blood vessels move blood throughout your body and assists in releasing hormones.

## Food Sources
*   **Dairy Products:** Milk, yogurt, and cheese are excellent sources often used in high-calorie and high-protein diets.
*   **Information not in sources:** Dark leafy green vegetables like kale, broccoli, and collard greens.
*   **Information not in sources:** Canned fish with soft bones, such as sardines and salmon.
*   **Information not in sources:** Fortified foods, including certain breakfast cereals, orange juices, and plant-based milk alternatives.

## Deficiency
*   An inability to maintain proper nutrient levels puts you at an increased risk for malnutrition during cancer treatment.
*   Not getting enough essential minerals can contribute to feeling weak or having low energy.
*   **Information not in sources:** Long-term deficiency can lead to low bone mass (osteopenia) and an increased risk of bone fractures (osteoporosis).
*   **Information not in sources:** Severe lack of calcium can cause numbness or tingling in the fingers and muscle cramps.

## Daily Requirements
*   General guidance for cancer patients is to eat a wide variety of nutrient-dense foods to maintain a healthy weight.
*   Your specific calcium needs depend on your age, gender, and the specific type of cancer treatment you are receiving.
*   Oncology registered dietitians provide 1:1 counseling to help determine your personalized requirements.

## Supplement Considerations
*   Always talk to your oncologist or oncology dietitian before starting any calcium supplements.
*   Some supplements may interfere with certain medications or how your body responds to treatment.
*   Your care team can help you decide if a supplement is a safe part of your scientifically-supported nutrition plan.

## Key Facts
*   Cancer was a leading cause of death globally in 2024, accounting for nearly **10 million deaths**.
*   Approximately **38% of cancers** can be prevented through healthy lifestyle choices, including a healthy diet.
*   Nutritional care is recognized as a **human right** for all patients to support their dignity and health.
*   Low fruit and vegetable intake is a major modifiable risk factor for many types of cancer.
*   Early nutritional intervention can lead to an **easier recovery** and improved quality of life.
