# Preventing Foodborne Illness in Cancer Patients

## Introduction
*   Maintaining proper nutrition is essential before, during, and after your cancer treatment journey.
*   Procedures like chemotherapy, radiation, and surgery can lower your appetite and energy, increasing the risk of malnutrition.
*   Specialized nutrition care helps you stay strong so you can better cope with the challenges of treatment.

## What is Foodborne Illness?
*   Foodborne illness, commonly known as food poisoning, occurs when you eat food contaminated with harmful germs or toxins.
*   **Information not in sources:** These illnesses can range from mild stomach upset to severe infections that require hospital care.

## Common Causes
*   Biological agents, such as certain viruses, bacteria, or parasites.
*   Chemical contaminants like aflatoxin, which is a common food contaminant.
*   Contaminants in drinking water, such as arsenic.

## Common Symptoms
*   Feeling sick to your stomach or nauseated.
*   Vomiting.
*   Diarrhea or stomach cramps.
*   **Information not in sources:** Other common signs include fever, chills, and physical weakness.

## People at Higher Risk
*   Individuals receiving chemotherapy, radiation therapy, or biological immunotherapy.
*   Patients recovering from surgery, which can significantly change your nutrition needs.
*   Cancer treatments can weaken your immune system, making it harder for your body to fight off germs found in food.

## How to Prevent Foodborne Illness
*   **Clean:** Wash your hands, utensils, and kitchen surfaces often to remove harmful bacteria.
*   **Separate:** Keep raw meats, poultry, and eggs away from ready-to-eat foods to prevent cross-contamination.
*   **Cook:** Heat all foods to the proper internal temperature to kill biological contaminants.
*   **Chill:** Refrigerate or freeze leftovers and perishables promptly to stop germs from growing.

## Safe Food Choices
*   Focus on high-calorie and high-protein foods to help rebuild tissues harmed by treatment.
*   Eat a wide variety of nutrient-dense foods to ensure you get enough vitamins and minerals.
*   Drink plenty of safe fluids, as they are essential for your body to function correctly.

## When to Seek Medical Help
*   If you are having a difficult time managing side effects like severe vomiting or diarrhea.
*   If you notice unplanned weight loss or a significant drop in your energy levels.
*   Ask your treatment team for a nutrition referral to speak with a registered dietitian if you have safety concerns.

## Key Facts
*   Cancer is a leading cause of death worldwide, accounting for nearly 10 million lives lost in 2024.
*   Approximately 38% of cancers are preventable through healthy lifestyle choices, including a healthy diet.
*   Nutritional care is recognized as a human right for all patients by organizations like ESPEN.
*   Early detection and appropriate treatment give many common cancers a high chance of being cured.
