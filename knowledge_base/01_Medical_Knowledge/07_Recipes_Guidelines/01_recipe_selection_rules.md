# Recipe Selection Rules for Cancer Nutrition Chatbot

## Introduction
*   **Oncology registered dietitians** provide comprehensive, customized, and scientifically-supported nutrition care to help you optimize your health.
*   Nutritional care is a **fundamental human right** for all patients, supporting your dignity and health throughout your journey.
*   Proper nutrition is a vital part of care before, during, and after cancer treatment.

## Purpose of Recipe Selection
*   **Weight Maintenance:** The primary goal is to help you maintain a healthy weight.
*   **Tissue Repair:** Recommending protein-rich recipes helps rebuild tissues that may be harmed by treatments.
*   **Energy Levels:** Correct food choices provide the energy needed to cope with the challenges of treatment.
*   **Quality of Life:** Improving nutrition supports your physical and psychosocial well-being.
*   **Malnutrition Prevention:** Targeted recipes reduce the risk of malnutrition caused by treatment side effects.

## Factors Considered Before Recommending a Recipe

### Patient's Symptoms
*   **Mouth and Throat Issues:** Presence of mouth sores, dry mouth, or painful swallowing.
*   **Digestive Changes:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, gas, or bloating.
*   **Taste Changes:** Alterations in how food tastes, such as metallic or bitter sensations.
*   **Appetite Status:** Whether the patient has a decreased or increased appetite.

### Treatment Stage
*   **Active Treatment:** Currently receiving chemotherapy, radiation, hormone therapy, or biological immunotherapy.
*   **Surgical Phase:** Nutrition needs before surgery to build strength or after surgery for recovery.
*   **Survivorship:** Focus on cancer prevention and long-term recovery.

### Nutritional Requirements
*   **Weight Status:** Whether the patient is experiencing undesirable weight loss or unplanned weight gain.
*   **Specific Needs:** High-calorie and high-protein requirements to minimize weight changes.
*   **Nutrient Deficiencies:** Recommending foods that help improve specific nutrient gaps.

### Food Preferences
*   **Informed Choices:** Helping patients make choices that they find appealing during a difficult time.
*   **Emotional Impact:** Recognizing that food has a mental and emotional impact on recovery.

### Dietary Restrictions
*   **Medical Necessity:** Specialized needs like tube feeding or intravenous nutrition support.
*   **Safety:** Adjusting for a weakened immune system during intensive treatments.

### Available Ingredients
*   **Nutrient Density:** Prioritizing recipes with fruits, vegetables, and essential vitamins/minerals.
*   **Safe Sources:** Using safe drinking water and avoiding contaminated foods.

## Recipe Suitability Rules
*   **Match Texture to Side Effects:** Recommend liquid or soft-food recipes for dry mouth, mouth sores, or swallowing problems.
*   **Match Flavor to Taste Changes:** Provide options that can be adjusted with seasonings if food tastes different.
*   **Match Complexity to Energy:** Suggest simpler, easy-to-prepare recipes for patients with low energy.
*   **Match Ingredients to Digestion:** Suggest bland, easy-to-digest foods for nausea and vomiting.

## Recipe Priority Rules
*   **High-Calorie/High-Protein Priority:** Rank these recipes higher for patients feeling weak, underweight, or losing weight.
*   **Variety Priority:** Rank recipes that include a wide variety of food groups higher to ensure balanced nutrient intake.
*   **Hydration Priority:** Prioritize recipes with high fluid content (like soups or smoothies) for patients needing to replace lost fluids.

## When Not to Recommend a Recipe
*   **Alcohol Contained:** Do not recommend recipes containing alcohol, as reducing consumption is a key factor in lowering cancer risk.
*   **Known Contaminants:** Avoid ingredients high in risk for **aflatoxin** (a food contaminant) or **arsenic** (a water contaminant).
*   **Trigger Foods:** Do not suggest greasy or fried foods for patients currently experiencing nausea.
*   **Immune Risk:** Avoid raw or undercooked animal products if the patient is currently immunocompromised.

## Key Facts
*   Cancer caused nearly **10 million deaths** globally in 2024, or roughly one in six deaths.
*   Approximately **38% of cancers are preventable** through healthy lifestyle choices, including a healthy diet.
*   The most common cancers diagnosed worldwide are **lung, breast, colon and rectum, and prostate**.
*   Nearly a quarter of cancer deaths are linked to modifiable risks like low fruit and vegetable intake and alcohol use.
*   Many cancers have a high chance of being cured if they are **detected early** and treated properly.
