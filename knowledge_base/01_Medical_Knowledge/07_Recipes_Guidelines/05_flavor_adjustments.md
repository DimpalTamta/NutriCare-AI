# Flavor Adjustments for Cancer Patients

## Introduction
*   Eating well is essential before, during, and after your cancer treatment.
*   The main goal of your diet is to keep up your energy and maintain a healthy weight.
*   Eating a wide variety of foods helps your body rebuild tissues that treatment may harm.
*   Oncology registered dietitians are experts who can help you make a plan that fits your needs.

## Why Flavor Changes Occur
*   Treatments like chemotherapy and radiation can change the way you taste food.
*   These procedures can make food taste different, bland, or even unpleasant.
*   Side effects can lead to a loss of appetite and put you at risk for weight loss.

## Managing Taste Changes
*   Experimenting with different flavors can help you find foods that still taste good.
*   Personalized counseling from a dietitian can help you address specific taste concerns.
*   Small, frequent meals may be easier to handle than three large ones if flavors seem overwhelming.

## Reducing Strong Smells
*   Strong food odors can sometimes trigger nausea or vomiting during treatment.
*   Eating foods cold or at room temperature can help because they have less of a smell.
*   Staying away from the kitchen while food is being cooked can help you avoid strong scents.

## Increasing Flavor Naturally
*   Marinating meats or plant proteins can add moisture and enhance their flavor.
*   Adding healthy fats, like oils or butter, can improve taste and add much-needed calories.
*   Using sauces, gravies, or dressings can help if your mouth feels dry or food tastes bland.

## Using Herbs
*   Fresh or dried herbs can add a lot of flavor without being too heavy.
*   Herbs like basil, parsley, or rosemary can make proteins and vegetables more appealing.
*   Trying different herb combinations can help you find new favorites when your taste buds change.

## Using Mild Spices
*   Mild spices can help mask "off" flavors and make meals more exciting.
*   Avoid very hot or spicy flavors if you have mouth sores or a sore throat.
*   Cinnamon or ginger can add a pleasant warmth to foods and may help with nausea.

## Managing Metallic Taste
*   Many patients experience a metallic taste in their mouth during chemotherapy.
*   Try using plastic forks and spoons instead of metal ones to reduce this sensation.
*   Rinsing your mouth with water or a mild baking soda solution before eating can help clear the taste.

## Managing Bitter Taste
*   Adding a small amount of sweetener, like honey or sugar, can help balance out bitterness.
*   Tart flavors like lemon or lime can help cut through a bitter taste in your mouth.
*   Experimenting with different cooking methods, like roasting, can also change how bitterness is perceived.

## Managing Sweet Taste Changes
*   Some patients find that sweet foods taste too sugary or sickly during treatment.
*   Adding a pinch of salt or a squeeze of lemon juice can help tone down excessive sweetness.
*   Diluting sweet drinks with water or ice can make them easier to enjoy.

## Foods to Avoid
*   Avoid very spicy or acidic foods if you have mouth sores, as they can sting.
*   Limit greasy, heavy, or fried foods if you are feeling nauseated or have stomach issues.
*   Tobacco and alcohol should be avoided, as they are major risk factors for cancer and can ruin your sense of taste.

## Key Facts
*   Cancer is a leading cause of death globally, accounting for nearly 10 million deaths in 2024.
*   About 37% to 38% of new cancer cases can be prevented by following a healthy diet and lifestyle.
*   Nutritional care is recognized as a human right for all patients undergoing treatment.
