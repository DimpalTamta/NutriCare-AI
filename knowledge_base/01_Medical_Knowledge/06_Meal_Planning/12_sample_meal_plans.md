# Sample Meal Plans for Cancer Patients

## Introduction
*   **Nutrition is a vital part** of your journey before, during, and after cancer treatment.
*   Oncology registered dietitians specialize in helping you optimize your health and make informed choices about your food.
*   Providing specialized nutritional care is recognized as a **human right** for all patients.

## Purpose of Sample Meal Plans
*   The primary goal during treatment is to **maintain a healthy weight** and prevent malnutrition.
*   These examples show how to include high-calorie and high-protein foods to support your strength.
*   Sample plans demonstrate how to use **small, frequent meals** to manage side effects.

## Breakfast Examples
*   Soft scrambled eggs prepared with whole milk or added cheese.
*   Oatmeal or Cream of Wheat made with milk instead of water.
*   Full-fat Greek yogurt topped with soft fruit and a sprinkle of nuts.
*   Whole-wheat toast with mashed avocado or a thick layer of nut butter.

## Lunch Examples
*   Creamy bean, lentil, or split pea soups.
*   Chicken, tuna, or egg salad made with extra mayonnaise on soft bread.
*   Quinoa or brown rice bowls with lean protein and well-cooked vegetables.
*   Cottage cheese paired with canned peaches or soft melon.

## Dinner Examples
*   Baked or stewed chicken or fish served with gravies or moist sauces.
*   Ground meat dishes like meatloaf, shepherd's pie, or soft tacos.
*   Whole-wheat pasta tossed with olive oil, lean protein, and tender vegetables.
*   Slow-cooked pot roast or vegetable stews with very soft textures.

## Snack Examples
*   Cheese sticks, cubes, or slices.
*   Hard-boiled eggs.
*   A handful of nuts, seeds, or dried fruit.
*   High-protein puddings or full-fat yogurt.

## Meal Plans for Different Symptoms
*   **For Nausea:** Focus on bland, dry foods like crackers or plain toast.
*   **For Sore Mouth/Throat:** Choose very soft, moist foods and add extra sauces to make swallowing easier.
*   **For Taste Changes:** Experiment with different spices and use plastic utensils if food tastes metallic.
*   **For Decreased Appetite:** Prioritize small "power" snacks every few hours rather than large meals.

## Meal Plans for High-Protein Needs
*   Incorporate lean meats, poultry, fish, eggs, and dairy at every meal.
*   Add protein-rich beans or lentils to soups, salads, and casseroles.
*   Use liquid nutritional supplements if you find it difficult to eat enough solid protein.

## Meal Plans for High-Calorie Needs
*   Always choose **full-fat versions** of dairy products like milk, yogurt, and cheese.
*   Add healthy fats like olive oil, avocado, or nut butters to your grains and vegetables.
*   Focus on energy-dense snacks such as nuts, seeds, and dried fruits.

## Hydration Schedule
*   Fluids are essential for your body to function correctly during treatment.
*   Take **small, frequent sips** throughout the day rather than drinking large amounts at once.
*   If you feel full quickly, drink most of your fluids between meals instead of during them.
*   Ensure all water comes from a **safe, treated source** to avoid contaminants like arsenic.

## Important Notes
*   Always consult your oncology team before making major changes to your diet.
*   Ask for a **nutrition referral** to speak with a registered dietitian for a plan tailored to your medical history.
*   Follow strict **food safety guidelines** to prevent infections while your immune system is weak.

## Key Facts
*   Cancer was a leading cause of death in 2024, accounting for nearly **10 million lives** globally.
*   Approximately **38% of cancers** are preventable through healthy lifestyle factors, including a healthy diet.
*   Maintaining a healthy weight is the **most important nutritional goal** during active treatment.
*   Low fruit and vegetable intake is a major modifiable risk factor for cancer worldwide.
