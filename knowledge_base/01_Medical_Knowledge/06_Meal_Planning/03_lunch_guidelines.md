# Healthy Lunch Guidelines for Cancer Patients

## Introduction
*   Oncology registered dietitians provide customized, scientifically-supported nutrition care to help you optimize your health.
*   Maintaining proper nutrition is vital before, during, and after cancer treatment.
*   Specialized nutrition care is recognized as a **human right** for every patient to support their dignity and health.

## Importance of Lunch
*   Lunch provides the **high-calorie and high-protein** intake needed to help maintain a healthy weight.
*   It supplies the energy required to cope with daily challenges and the physical stress of procedures like surgery, chemotherapy, or radiation.
*   Proper attention to midday nutrition assists in an **easier recovery**.

## Building a Balanced Lunch
*   The primary goal is to eat a wide variety of foods to **prevent malnutrition** and undesirable weight loss.
*   A balanced plate should include protein to rebuild tissues, carbohydrates for energy, and enough vitamins and minerals for body functioning.
*   Your lunch choices may differ from a standard "healthy diet" to focus specifically on calorie density if you are feeling weak.

## Protein Sources
*   Protein is essential for rebuilding tissues that cancer treatments may harm.
*   **Animal-Based:** Lean meats, poultry, fish, eggs, and dairy products.
*   **Plant-Based:** Beans and lentils are excellent high-protein options.
*   An oncology dietitian can help you improve **nutrient deficiencies** by choosing the right protein sources.

## Vegetable Choices
*   Eating a variety of vegetables is a key component of a healthy diet and helps reduce the overall cancer burden.
*   Low vegetable intake is a modifiable risk factor for many types of cancer.
*   If you have a decreased appetite, try incorporating vegetables into soups or stews to make them easier to consume.

## Whole Grain Options
*   Whole grains provide the carbohydrates necessary to maintain your energy levels throughout the day.
*   Options include whole-wheat bread for sandwiches, brown rice, or quinoa.
*   Grains can be a good base for high-calorie additions like healthy fats or proteins.

## Hydration During Lunch
*   Fluids are essential for your body to function correctly, especially during treatment.
*   Ensure you use **safe drinking water** to avoid contaminants like arsenic, which are known health risks.
*   If you are losing weight, consider nutrient-dense liquids like broths or liquid supplements instead of just water.

## Foods to Limit
*   **Alcohol:** Consumption should be avoided or significantly reduced to lower cancer risk.
*   **Contaminated Foods:** Be cautious of food contaminants like **aflatoxin**.
*   **Symptom Triggers:** Limit foods that cause you discomfort, such as very greasy foods if you are nauseated or high-gas foods like certain beans if you are bloated.

## Meal Planning Tips
*   **Manage Side Effects:** Plan lunches that are easy to eat if you are experiencing taste changes, dry mouth, or swallowing problems.
*   **Small, Frequent Meals:** If you cannot finish a full lunch, try eating several smaller portions throughout the afternoon.
*   **Get a Referral:** Ask your treatment team for a **nutrition referral** to speak with a registered dietitian for a personalized meal plan.

## Key Facts
*   Cancer was a leading cause of death globally in 2024, accounting for nearly **10 million deaths**.
*   Approximately **38% of cancers are preventable** through healthy lifestyle choices, including a healthy diet.
*   Lung, breast, and colorectal cancers are among the most common types diagnosed worldwide.
*   The most important nutritional goal during active treatment is to **maintain a healthy weight**.
