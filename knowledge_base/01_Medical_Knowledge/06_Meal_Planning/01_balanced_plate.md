# Building a Balanced Plate During Cancer Treatment

## Introduction
*   Oncology registered dietitians provide comprehensive and scientifically-supported care to help you optimize your health before, during, and after cancer treatment.
*   Proper nutrition is a vital part of your care, helping you make informed choices during a crucial time.
*   Specialized nutrition care is recognized as a human right for every patient to support their dignity and health.

## What is a Balanced Plate?
*   A balanced plate for cancer patients focuses on a wide variety of foods that provide the calories, protein, and nutrients your body needs to heal.
*   While a "healthy diet" usually focuses on fruits and vegetables, an oncology balanced plate often emphasizes high-calorie and high-protein foods to support your recovery.

## Why a Balanced Plate is Important for Cancer Patients
*   **Weight Maintenance:** The primary goal of nutrition during treatment is to maintain a healthy weight to stay strong.
*   **Tissue Repair:** Getting enough protein and nutrients helps your body rebuild tissues that may be harmed by chemotherapy, radiation, or surgery.
*   **Energy Maintenance:** A balanced intake provides the energy needed to cope with the daily challenges and side effects of treatment.
*   **Preventing Malnutrition:** Proper food choices reduce the risk of malnutrition, which can occur when procedures or medications cause a loss of appetite.

## Components of a Balanced Plate

### Protein
*   Protein is essential for healing and rebuilding the body's tissues during and after treatment.
*   Focus on high-protein sources such as lean meats, poultry, fish, eggs, dairy products, beans, and lentils.

### Whole Grains / Carbohydrates
*   Carbohydrates provide the primary fuel your body needs for energy.
*   Include options like whole-wheat bread, brown rice, oats, and quinoa to help maintain your weight.

### Fruits
*   Fruits provide essential vitamins, minerals, and antioxidants to support your immune system.
*   Try to include a variety of colorful fruits, as low fruit intake is a known risk factor for poor health.

### Vegetables
*   Vegetables are nutrient-dense and help support the body's basic functioning.
*   Focus on including different colors and types of vegetables every day to ensure a wide range of nutrients.

### Healthy Fats
*   Fats are a concentrated source of energy and are helpful for patients who need to eat more calories to maintain their weight.
*   Include healthy options like olive oil, avocados, nuts, and seeds in your meals.

### Fluids
*   Staying hydrated is essential for your body to function correctly during treatment.
*   Water is an excellent choice, but other fluids like broth or liquid supplements may be needed if you are losing weight.

## Adjusting the Plate Based on Symptoms
*   **Decreased Appetite:** Focus on high-calorie and high-protein "power" foods, and try eating smaller portions more frequently throughout the day.
*   **Nausea or Vomiting:** Choose bland foods and prioritize small, frequent sips of fluids to stay hydrated.
*   **Taste Changes:** If food tastes different or metallic, experiment with different flavors, spices, or temperatures to find what is palatable.
*   **Dry Mouth or Sore Throat:** Incorporate moist foods, sauces, and gravies to make swallowing easier and more comfortable.

## Portion Balance
*   Standard portion sizes may need to be ignored during treatment; the most important goal is simply to eat enough to maintain your weight.
*   If you are feeling weak or losing weight, your dietitian may recommend increasing the portion of protein and fats on your plate.

## Practical Tips
*   **Eat When Hungry:** If you have more appetite in the morning, make breakfast your largest and most nutrient-dense meal of the day.
*   **Keep Snacks Ready:** Have high-protein snacks like cheese, yogurt, or nuts easily available for when you feel able to eat.
*   **Stay Active:** If possible, light exercise can help improve your appetite and aid in digestion.
*   **Ask for Help:** Request a nutrition referral from your treatment team to get a personalized plan from an oncology registered dietitian.

## Key Facts
*   Cancer was a leading cause of death globally in 2024, accounting for nearly 10 million lives lost.
*   Approximately 38% of cancers are preventable through healthy lifestyle choices, which include eating a healthy diet rich in fruits and vegetables.
*   Low fruit and vegetable intake is a major modifiable risk factor for cancer worldwide.
*   Maintaining a healthy weight is the most important nutritional goal for individuals undergoing active treatment.
*   Nutritional care is recognized as a human right to support the physical and psychosocial well-being of patients.
