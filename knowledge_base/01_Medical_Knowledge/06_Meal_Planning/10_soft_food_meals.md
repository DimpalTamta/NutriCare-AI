# Soft Food Meals for Cancer Patients

## Introduction
* Oncology registered dietitians provide scientifically supported, customized care to help you optimize your health before, during, and after treatment.
* Nutrition is a vital part of your journey, and your care team is dedicated to helping you make informed choices during this crucial time.
* Specialized nutritional care is recognized as a human right for every patient to support their dignity and health.

## Who Needs Soft Foods?
* Patients experiencing side effects that impact eating, such as painful mouth sores or inflammation.
* Individuals suffering from dry mouth (xerostomia), which makes chewing and swallowing difficult.
* Patients with swallowing problems (dysphagia) caused by the cancer itself or its treatment.
* Those recovering from certain surgeries, particularly head and neck procedures, which change nutrition needs.

## Benefits
* **Maintains Weight:** Helps you achieve the primary goal of staying at a healthy weight during treatment.
* **Supports Healing:** Provides the protein needed to rebuild body tissues harmed by chemotherapy or radiation.
* **Increases Comfort:** Soft textures reduce irritation in the mouth and throat, making it easier to eat even when you don't feel well.
* **Maintains Energy:** Ensures you get enough calories to cope with the daily challenges and physical stress of treatment.

## Suitable Foods
* **Protein:** Soft scrambled eggs, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, and well-cooked beans or lentils.
* **Grains:** Oatmeal, Cream of Wheat, well-cooked pasta, or soft bread.
* **Fruits/Vegetables:** Mashed potatoes, applesauce, fruit smoothies, or vegetables steamed until tender.
* **Moistening Agents:** Use gravies, sauces, broths, and dressings liberally to make every bite easier to swallow.

## Foods to Avoid
* **Information not in sources:** Dry or crunchy foods like chips, crackers, or hard nuts that can scratch the mouth or throat.
* **Information not in sources:** Very spicy or acidic foods (like citrus or vinegar) that may sting mouth sores.
* **Information not in sources:** Rough textures such as raw vegetables, tough meats, or seedy fruits.

## Cooking Methods
* **Slow Cooking:** Prepare stews, soups, or casseroles where meats and vegetables become very tender.
* **Blending:** Use a blender or food processor to turn solid meals or fruits into smooth purees and smoothies.
* **Mashing:** Cook foods like potatoes, squash, or carrots until they can be easily mashed with a fork.

## Sample Meal Ideas
* **Breakfast:** Soft oatmeal made with whole milk for extra calories and topped with mashed banana.
* **Lunch:** Creamy lentil soup or soft egg salad with plenty of mayonnaise for moisture.
* **Dinner:** Ground meat served with a rich gravy and a side of mashed vegetables.
* **Snack:** A high-protein smoothie with yogurt and fruit, or a bowl of cottage cheese.

## Eating Tips
* **Small, Frequent Portions:** If large meals are overwhelming, try eating several smaller portions throughout the day.
* **Manage Taste Changes:** If food tastes metallic, try using plastic utensils instead of metal ones.
* **Sip Fluids:** Take small, frequent sips of water or other fluids to keep your mouth moist and stay hydrated.
* **Get a Referral:** Ask your treatment team for a nutrition referral to speak 1:1 with an oncology dietitian for a personalized plan.

## Key Facts
* Cancer was a leading cause of death globally in 2024, accounting for nearly 10 million lives lost.
* Approximately 38% of cancers are currently preventable through healthy lifestyle choices, including a healthy diet.
* Many common cancers have a high chance of being cured if they are detected early and treated according to best practices.
* Low fruit and vegetable intake is a major modifiable risk factor for cancer globally.
