# Portion Control During Cancer Treatment

## Introduction
*   Proper nutrition is a vital part of your journey before, during, and after cancer treatment.
*   Oncology registered dietitians provide scientifically-supported care to help you optimize your health and make informed choices.
*   Maintaining a healthy weight is the primary goal for most individuals undergoing treatment.

## What is Portion Control?
*   Portion control in cancer care means adjusting how much you eat at one time to match your body's needs and your ability to tolerate food.
*   Unlike standard diets, portion sizes for cancer patients are often modified to manage treatment-related side effects.

## Why Portion Control is Important
*   **Weight Maintenance:** Proper portions help you maintain a healthy weight and avoid undesirable weight loss.
*   **Healing:** Eating enough allows your body to rebuild tissues harmed by chemotherapy, radiation, or surgery.
*   **Energy Levels:** Correct portions provide the energy needed to cope with the physical and mental challenges of treatment.
*   **Preventing Malnutrition:** Managing how much you eat reduces the risk of malnutrition caused by a loss of appetite.

## Factors Affecting Portion Size
*   **Decreased Appetite:** Treatment may make it difficult to eat large amounts of food at once.
*   **Treatment Side Effects:** Nausea, vomiting, gas, and bloating can make standard portions feel overwhelming.
*   **Mouth and Throat Issues:** Dry mouth, sores, or swallowing problems may require smaller, softer portions.
*   **Increased Needs:** Your body may require larger amounts of protein and calories than usual to recover from surgery or therapy.

## Signs of Eating Too Much
*   **Discomfort:** Feeling overly full, bloated, or experiencing increased gas after a meal.
*   **Nausea:** A sudden increase in nausea or an urge to vomit if the stomach is too full.
*   **Unplanned Weight Gain:** Gaining weight rapidly or unexpectedly, which may occur with certain medications.

## Signs of Eating Too Little
*   **Weight Loss:** Unplanned or undesirable weight loss is a major sign that portions are too small.
*   **Weakness:** Feeling physically weak or having very low energy to complete daily tasks.
*   **Slow Recovery:** Tissues taking a long time to heal after surgery or treatment sessions.
*   **Malnutrition:** A significant drop in nutrient levels that can affect your overall health and treatment success.

## Practical Portion Tips
*   **Mini-Meals:** Try eating smaller, more frequent portions throughout the day instead of three large meals.
*   **Focus on Density:** Choose high-calorie and high-protein "power" foods so that even small portions provide lots of energy.
*   **Morning Priority:** If your appetite is strongest in the morning, make breakfast your largest portion of the day.
*   **Stay Active:** Light exercise can help improve your appetite and aid in digestion, making it easier to eat proper portions.
*   **Seek Help:** Ask your oncology team for a referral to a registered dietitian for personalized portion guidance.

## Key Facts
*   Cancer was a leading cause of death globally in 2024, accounting for nearly **10 million deaths**.
*   Approximately **38% of cancers are preventable** through healthy lifestyle choices, including a healthy diet.
*   Nutritional care is recognized as a **human right** for all patients to support their dignity and health.
*   Low fruit and vegetable intake is a major modifiable risk factor for many types of cancer.
